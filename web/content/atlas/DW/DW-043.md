When a major project concludes, users want to clear their screen without permanently destroying the specific collection of open URLs, documents, reference files, and layouts used during that project. Standard operating systems offer no "Archive Workspace" snapshot feature, leaving users with a binary choice: leave everything open or close it all and rely on browser history/file paths to find it again later.

**Human Impact**

*   Accumulation of persistent visual clutter out of fear of losing references.
*   Difficulty retrieving exact research contexts months after project completion.
*   Time lost manually bookmarking or compiling link lists before closing project windows.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Wrapping up long-term research, client deliverables, or seasonal projects.
*   Needing to pause a project for several weeks with the intention of resuming later.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users keep dozens of tabs and documents open for weeks after active work has ended, explicitly stating they are afraid they will "never find all these specific pages again" if they close the windows.

**Research Notes**

*   Identifies the need for state-level workspace snapshots and archival mechanisms.
