As users perform complex tasks, they routinely open multiple applications, browser windows, utility programs, and documents on a single desktop workspace. Over time, overlapping windows completely obscure lower layers, making it increasingly difficult to visually identify or select a target application window. Users must resort to manual cycling, minimizing, or clicking through stacked taskbar items to find what they need, causing friction and delaying workflow execution.

**Human Impact**

*   Time lost searching through stacked or overlapped application windows.
*   Disruption of focus and task momentum.
*   Increased mental effort required to maintain mental models of open software.
*   Accidental selection of incorrect application instances.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Performing multitasking operations involving browsers, editors, and communication tools.
*   Working on single-monitor displays with dense window overlaps.
*   Accumulating application windows across long, uninterrupted work sessions.

**Evidence / Observation**

Observed across major desktop OS environments. When the number of open windows exceeds what can be clearly arranged visually on screen, users spend significant time employing application switchers, inspecting taskbar/dock previews, or moving windows aside to expose underlying applications.
