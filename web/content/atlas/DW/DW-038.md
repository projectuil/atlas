Once a task or project is finished, the collection of open windows, tabs, and folders associated with it remains on screen in the exact same state as active tasks. Because operating systems lack a concept of task completion, finished work lingers on the desktop alongside active projects, clogging the user's workspace.

**Human Impact**

*   Accumulation of "dead" workspaces that pollute daily operations.
*   Confusion over whether a lingering project requires further action.
*   Friction and hesitation when determining what can be safely closed or cleared.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Finishing a meeting, submitting a deliverable, or completing a research thread.
*   Working sequentially through multiple distinct tickets or tasks in a single shift.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users leave finished project configurations open for days after completion simply because there is no structured "Mark as Completed" or "Archive Workspace" mechanism in standard OSs.

**Research Notes**

*   Highlights lifecycle management gaps in contemporary desktop operating systems.
