When users are away from their workspace for extended periods (e.g., weekend breaks, vacations, multi-day illness), returning to an open desktop workspace presents severe re-orientation challenges. Without context summaries or mental breadcrumbs, users must spend significant cognitive effort reconstructing what they were doing, what step was next, and why specific files were left open.

**Human Impact**

*   Prolonged ramp-up time when returning to work after time off.
*   Mental anxiety upon facing a complex, static desktop layout with forgotten context.
*   Elevated risk of duplicating work or making wrong assumptions about task status.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Starting work on Monday morning after leaving windows open on Friday evening.
*   Returning to active projects following a vacation or multi-day conference.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users frequently stare at open desktops for several minutes upon returning from breaks, clicking through tabs and inspecting open documents to rebuild their previous mental model.

**Research Notes**

*   Explores temporal memory decay and the need for desktop context-resumption summaries.
