Desktop GUI paradigms (taskbars, windows, docks) scale poorly beyond a threshold of active windows (typically 7-10 open applications). Beyond this point, icons compress, taskbar labels disappear, and spatial arrangements breakdown entirely, causing exponential increases in search friction.

**Human Impact**

*   Collapse of organizational structures under high application loads.
*   Drastic drop in navigation speed and workspace legibility.
*   Elevated stress levels due to unmanageable screen state.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Mid-day peak activity during complex multi-stage projects.
*   Power users running specialized multi-tool pipelines.

**Evidence / Observation**

As active window count rises above 10, taskbar icons shrink to unreadable sizes without text labels, forcing users to hover over every single icon sequentially to identify windows.

**Research Notes**

*   Demonstrates structural non-linearity in desktop UI scaling.
