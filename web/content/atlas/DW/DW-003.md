Modern office workflows demand the simultaneous use of email, chat apps, spreadsheets, project management boards, and document editors. Desktop operating systems handle each of these as isolated software packages rather than a cohesive daily workspace. Users are forced to act as manual integrators, continuously jumping back and forth across fragmented tools to fulfill routine daily operations.

**Human Impact**

*   High cognitive overhead due to frequent task-switching.
*   Accumulated fatigue from manual copy-paste and navigation sequences.
*   Higher risk of missing updates or context in secondary applications.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Performing routine office tasks requiring information synthesis across platforms.
*   Cross-referencing communications (Slack/Teams/Email) while drafting reports or spreadsheets.

**Evidence / Observation**

Office workers consistently execute dozens to hundreds of window-switching interactions per hour simply to pull information from one isolated application and paste or verify it in another.

**Research Notes**

*   Demonstrates the core gap between task-level objectives and application-level system design.
*   Forms a primary foundation for workspace unification models.
