Users often fall into one of two productivity traps: either tolerating destructive clutter or over-investing time into meticulously arranging, tiling, color-coding, and tweaking window positions. Users struggle to identify the tipping point where the time spent maintaining workspace organization exceeds the productivity benefits derived from it.

**Human Impact**

*   Time loss caused by compulsory window adjustment and layout tinkering.
*   Misguided perception of productivity through organization rather than task execution.
*   Friction when rigid organizational systems break down under real-time pressures.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Setting up elaborate virtual desktop rules, custom tiling managers, or manual window grids.
*   Experiencing task procrastination disguised as workspace optimization.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users spend significant operational time aligning borders or arranging windows perfectly, only for the layout to be dismantled by the next incoming pop-up or workflow shift.

**Research Notes**

*   Explores the psychological inflection point between functional order and organizational overhead.
