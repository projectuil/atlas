As users navigate complex workflows, they open utility windows or browser tabs as temporary reminders or steps for pending sub-tasks. When returning to the desktop later, the original intent or memory trigger for these open windows is forgotten, leaving the user hesitant to close them out of fear of discarding unfinished work.

**Human Impact**

*   Hesitation and cognitive friction when auditing open windows.
*   Unnecessary retention of stale windows that clutter the desktop.
*   Chronic low-grade anxiety over potentially missed or incomplete tasks.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Leaving work open overnight or taking long breaks during the day.
*   Handling frequent, high-volume micro-tasks alongside deep work.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users regularly hover over open windows or browser tabs asking, "Why did I open this?" before eventually closing them without action or leaving them open indefinitely.

**Research Notes**

*   Demonstrates the misuse of open windows as visual memory proxies without explicit metadata.
