Users frequently believe they are making objective decisions while remaining unaware of the cognitive biases influencing their reasoning. Personal experiences, prior beliefs, emotional responses, and mental shortcuts can unconsciously shape how information is interpreted and evaluated. Because these influences often operate without conscious awareness, users struggle to recognize when their judgment has become biased.

**Human Impact**

*   Reduced decision accuracy.
*   Increased reasoning errors.
*   Higher cognitive load.
*   Overconfidence in conclusions.
*   Lower decision quality.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Business decision-making.
*   Recruitment and hiring.
*   Cybersecurity analysis.
*   Academic research.
*   Everyday personal decisions.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed across both professional and personal decision-making. Users often believe their conclusions are based solely on evidence while unintentionally favoring information that aligns with existing beliefs or experiences. Existing information systems primarily provide access to evidence but offer limited support for helping users recognize the cognitive biases that influence their interpretation of that evidence.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on awareness of cognitive bias during reasoning.
*   Highlights unconscious influences on decision-making.
*   Related to psychology, critical thinking, and evidence evaluation.
