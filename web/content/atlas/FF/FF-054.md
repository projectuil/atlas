Users frequently encounter situations where multiple information sources provide conflicting explanations, recommendations, or conclusions. Although each source may appear credible, users often struggle to determine which one should be trusted. Without a reliable way to evaluate source credibility, users experience uncertainty, delay decisions, or make choices based on familiarity rather than evidence.

**Human Impact**

*   Increased uncertainty.
*   Delayed decision-making.
*   Higher cognitive load.
*   Reduced confidence.
*   Risk of incorrect decisions.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Online research.
*   Medical information searches.
*   Cybersecurity investigations.
*   Financial planning.
*   Academic study.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is frequently observed when users compare information from multiple books, websites, experts, research papers, or artificial intelligence systems. Users often recognize contradictions between sources but lack sufficient context to evaluate which source is more reliable for the specific situation. Existing information systems provide access to diverse viewpoints but offer limited support for helping users assess credibility when authoritative sources disagree.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on evaluating source credibility rather than information accuracy.
*   Builds upon information prioritization introduced in FF-053.
*   Related to trust, evidence evaluation, and decision confidence.
