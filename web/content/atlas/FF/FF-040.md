The same information is often distributed across multiple files in different formats, versions, or locations. Although the files appear different, they communicate substantially the same knowledge. Users frequently fail to recognize this overlap, resulting in repeated reading, duplicate editing, and unnecessary information management.

**Human Impact**

*   Duplicate work.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Longer information retrieval.
*   Knowledge fragmentation.
*   Reduced productivity.

**Context**

Commonly occurs in:

*   Research documentation.
*   Business documentation.
*   Software documentation.
*   Academic work.
*   Personal knowledge collections.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is commonly observed in projects that evolve over time. Information initially created in one document is often summarized, copied, or incorporated into additional files. Users later treat these files as independent resources because traditional file systems identify files rather than the knowledge they contain.

**Research Notes**

*   Contributes to **PAT-004 — Storage Without Understanding.**
*   Highlights the distinction between files and knowledge.
*   Related to information duplication and long-term knowledge organization.
