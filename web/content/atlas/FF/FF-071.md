Users frequently encounter conclusions supported by varying forms of evidence, including observations, expert opinions, statistical data, and research findings. Although the evidence may appear convincing, users often struggle to evaluate its quality, reliability, completeness, or relevance. This uncertainty makes it difficult to determine how strongly a conclusion should influence future decisions.

**Human Impact**

*   Reduced decision quality.
*   Increased uncertainty.
*   Higher cognitive load.
*   Difficulty comparing evidence.
*   Lower confidence.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Scientific research.
*   Medical decision-making.
*   Cybersecurity investigations.
*   Business analysis.
*   Public policy.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed when users evaluate competing claims supported by different forms of evidence. Users often recognize that evidence exists but struggle to assess whether it is sufficiently reliable or applicable to the situation. Existing information systems provide access to evidence but offer limited support for evaluating its overall quality and strength.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on evaluating evidence quality rather than information credibility.
*   Highlights differences between evidence strength and evidence availability.
*   Related to critical thinking, reasoning, and decision analysis.
