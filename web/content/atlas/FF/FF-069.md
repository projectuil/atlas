Users frequently acquire knowledge within one domain, project, or learning environment but struggle to apply that knowledge in different situations. Although the underlying principles remain relevant, differences in terminology, context, or presentation often prevent users from recognizing that previously learned knowledge can be reused. As a result, users repeatedly solve similar problems instead of transferring existing understanding.

**Human Impact**

*   Repeated learning effort.
*   Reduced problem-solving efficiency.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Slower skill development.
*   Limited knowledge utilization.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Education.
*   Software development.
*   Cybersecurity.
*   Engineering.
*   Workplace training.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed when users encounter unfamiliar situations that share underlying principles with previous experiences. Although users possess relevant knowledge, they often fail to recognize its applicability because the new context appears different on the surface. Existing learning systems typically organize knowledge by subject rather than by transferable principles, providing limited support for cross-context application.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on applying knowledge across different contexts.
*   Highlights transferability rather than knowledge acquisition.
*   Related to learning, abstraction, and problem-solving.
