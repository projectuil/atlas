Users frequently consume information without clearly distinguishing between verified facts, personal opinions, and speculative claims. Since these forms of information are often presented together, users may assign equal credibility to each despite their differing levels of evidence. This uncertainty makes it difficult to develop accurate understanding and informed decisions.

**Human Impact**

*   Increased uncertainty.
*   Misinterpretation of information.
*   Reduced decision quality.
*   Higher cognitive load.
*   Lower confidence.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   News consumption.
*   Social media.
*   Academic research.
*   Online learning.
*   Artificial intelligence interactions.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is frequently observed when users gather information from multiple digital sources. While factual statements, interpretations, and predictions often appear within the same content, users receive limited guidance for distinguishing between them. Existing information platforms primarily present content without consistently identifying the evidential nature of individual claims.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on classifying information rather than evaluating source credibility.
*   Distinguishes evidence-based knowledge from interpretation.
*   Related to critical thinking, media literacy, and information evaluation.
