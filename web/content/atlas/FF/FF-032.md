Users often reopen the same file multiple times over days, weeks, or months but later cannot remember why they repeatedly accessed it. While file systems may record modification dates or recent activity, they do not preserve the purpose behind each reopening. As a result, users lose the historical context surrounding their interactions with the file.

**Human Impact**

*   Time lost rebuilding context.
*   Interrupted workflow.
*   Increased cognitive effort.
*   Reduced understanding of previous work.
*   Lower productivity.

**Context**

Commonly occurs in:

*   Long-term research projects.
*   Software development.
*   Academic work.
*   Business documentation.
*   Personal knowledge repositories.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is frequently observed when users revisit long-lived documents. Although they recognize that a file has been opened many times, they often cannot remember what was accomplished during each session or why the file required repeated attention. Existing file systems primarily record access information rather than preserving the intent behind user interactions.

**Research Notes**

*   Contributes to **PAT-001 — Context Loss Over Time.**
*   Distinguishes activity history from contextual understanding.
*   Related to long-term knowledge management and workflow continuity.
