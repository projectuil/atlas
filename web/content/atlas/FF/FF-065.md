Decision-making often requires users to evaluate multiple pieces of knowledge that vary in importance, reliability, urgency, or relevance. Users frequently struggle to determine which information should carry the greatest influence on the final decision. Without a clear prioritization process, users may overemphasize less important information while overlooking evidence that has greater significance.

**Human Impact**

*   Reduced decision quality.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Slower decision-making.
*   Greater uncertainty.
*   Inconsistent outcomes.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Business strategy.
*   Medical diagnosis.
*   Cybersecurity investigations.
*   Financial planning.
*   Engineering design.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed when users evaluate complex decisions involving numerous factors. Although all available information may be relevant, users often struggle to determine which evidence deserves greater weight in the final decision. Existing decision-support systems provide access to information but offer limited support for helping users prioritize competing knowledge based on its significance.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on weighting information during decision-making.
*   Distinguishes prioritization from information discovery.
*   Related to reasoning, evidence evaluation, and strategic decision-making.
