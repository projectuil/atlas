Users frequently rely on knowledge acquired in the past without realizing that it may no longer be accurate or applicable. As technologies, regulations, research, and best practices evolve, previously correct information can become outdated. Users often struggle to recognize when their understanding requires revision, leading to decisions based on obsolete knowledge rather than current evidence.

**Human Impact**

*   Incorrect decision-making.
*   Reduced confidence.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Delayed adaptation.
*   Lower work quality.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Cybersecurity.
*   Software development.
*   Medical practice.
*   Academic research.
*   Legal and regulatory compliance.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed in rapidly evolving knowledge domains. Users often continue applying familiar concepts, procedures, or recommendations because they are unaware that newer information has replaced previous understanding. Existing information systems provide access to updated resources but offer limited support for helping users recognize when their existing knowledge has become outdated.

**Research Notes**

*   Completes the knowledge discovery stage by introducing knowledge currency.
*   Focuses on recognizing outdated knowledge rather than acquiring new knowledge.
*   Related to continuous learning, knowledge evolution, and decision quality.
