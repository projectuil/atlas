Users frequently stop working unexpectedly because of meetings, phone calls, deadlines, system shutdowns, or task switching. When they later return, they often cannot identify which files were interrupted midway through active work. Although the files remain accessible, there is little indication that work was paused before reaching a meaningful stopping point.

**Human Impact**

*   Time lost reconstructing previous work.
*   Interrupted workflow.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Forgotten progress.
*   Reduced productivity.

**Context**

Commonly occurs during:

*   Software development.
*   Research documentation.
*   Academic assignments.
*   Business reporting.
*   Creative content creation.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed when users are forced to stop working unexpectedly. Upon returning, they often reopen multiple files to determine where work was interrupted and what remained unfinished. Existing file systems preserve the files themselves but provide limited support for identifying documents that were abandoned during active work rather than intentionally completed.

**Research Notes**

*   Contributes to **PAT-002 — Memory Dependency.**
*   Focuses on interrupted work rather than unfinished work in general.
*   Related to workflow continuity and task resumption.
