Users often struggle to identify the files they were recently working on. Although files may have modification timestamps, users frequently remember their recent work as activities or tasks rather than exact filenames. This makes it difficult to quickly resume ongoing work after switching tasks, restarting a device, or returning after a period of inactivity.

**Human Impact**

*   Time lost locating recently used files.
*   Interrupted workflow.
*   Increased cognitive effort.
*   Slower task resumption.
*   Reduced productivity.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs after:**

*   Restarting a computer.
*   Returning to work the next day.
*   Switching between multiple projects.
*   Completing meetings.
*   Working across multiple applications.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is frequently observed in both personal and professional environments. Users often ask themselves questions such as "Which file was I working on?" or "Where did I stop yesterday?" Existing file systems provide limited support for reconstructing recent work based on user activity.

**Research Notes**

*   Contributes to PAT-001 — Context Loss Over Time.
*   Highlights the gap between chronological activity and file organization.
*   Closely related to workflow continuity and task resumption.
