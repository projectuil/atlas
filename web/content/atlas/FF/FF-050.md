Over months and years, file collections evolve through continuous creation, modification, reorganization, archiving, and collaboration. While users can often understand isolated parts of this ecosystem, they gradually lose the ability to maintain a coherent understanding of the entire collection as it changes. The challenge is not locating individual files but sustaining a comprehensive mental model of an ever-evolving information environment.

**Human Impact**

*   Declining understanding over time.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Slower decision-making.
*   Reduced confidence.
*   Lower long-term productivity.

**Context**

Commonly occurs in:

*   Long-term research repositories.
*   Enterprise software projects.
*   Academic knowledge bases.
*   Business documentation.
*   Personal digital archives.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed in mature digital ecosystems where hundreds or thousands of files accumulate over extended periods. Although users may successfully manage individual documents, they increasingly struggle to understand the collection as an interconnected and evolving whole. Traditional file systems preserve storage but provide limited support for maintaining long-term comprehension of dynamic information ecosystems.
