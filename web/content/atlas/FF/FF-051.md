Before making important decisions, users frequently struggle to determine whether they have acquired sufficient knowledge. Although they may have gathered information from multiple sources, they remain uncertain whether critical facts, perspectives, or evidence are still missing. This uncertainty often results in prolonged research, hesitation, delayed decisions, or reduced confidence because users cannot accurately assess the completeness of their understanding.

**Human Impact**

*   Delayed decision-making.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Repeated information gathering.
*   Reduced confidence.
*   Decision fatigue.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Academic research.
*   Business decision-making.
*   Software development.
*   Cybersecurity investigations.
*   Personal financial planning.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed whenever users make decisions involving uncertainty. Users often continue collecting information even after acquiring enough evidence because they cannot confidently determine whether additional knowledge would significantly improve the decision. Existing digital systems provide access to large amounts of information but offer limited support for helping users judge when their knowledge is sufficiently complete for confident decision-making.

**Research Notes**

*   Foundational Human Friction for Knowledge & Decision Management.
*   Focuses on evaluating knowledge sufficiency rather than acquiring knowledge.
*   Related to decision confidence, uncertainty, and cognitive assessment.
