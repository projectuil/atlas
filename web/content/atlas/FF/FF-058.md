Many real-world problems require knowledge from multiple disciplines rather than a single area of expertise. Users frequently possess the necessary knowledge within separate domains but struggle to recognize how these concepts relate to one another. As a result, they approach problems from a limited perspective and overlook solutions that emerge through combining knowledge across different fields.

**Human Impact**

*   Reduced problem-solving effectiveness.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Slower decision-making.
*   Missed solution opportunities.
*   Limited knowledge application.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Cybersecurity.
*   Software engineering.
*   Scientific research.
*   Business strategy.
*   Healthcare.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed when users encounter complex problems spanning multiple disciplines. Although relevant knowledge may already exist across different domains, users often treat each field independently and struggle to integrate concepts into a unified understanding. Existing information systems primarily organize knowledge by subject area, providing limited support for connecting related concepts across domains.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on integrating knowledge across disciplines.
*   Highlights relationships between separate knowledge domains.
*   Related to interdisciplinary learning, reasoning, and problem solving.
