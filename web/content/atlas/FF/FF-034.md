As projects evolve, files are created, modified, renamed, moved, and archived. Although the files remain available, users often struggle to reconstruct the sequence of events that explains how the project developed over time. This makes it difficult to understand previous decisions, revisit earlier work, or continue long-term projects with confidence.

**Human Impact**

*   Increased time understanding previous work.
*   Interrupted workflow.
*   Higher cognitive load.
*   Reduced project comprehension.
*   Slower decision-making.

**Context**

Commonly occurs in:

*   Long-term software projects.
*   Research repositories.
*   Business documentation.
*   Academic research.
*   Multi-phase collaborative projects.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is frequently observed when users revisit projects after weeks, months, or years. While individual files remain accessible, users often cannot determine the order in which important changes occurred or how the project evolved from its initial state. Existing file systems primarily preserve the current state of files rather than the broader narrative of project development.

**Research Notes**

*   Contributes to **PAT-001 — Context Loss Over Time.**
*   Highlights the distinction between preserving files and preserving project history.
*   Related to workflow continuity, long-term knowledge management, and project understanding.
