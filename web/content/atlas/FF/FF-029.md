Users often review multiple files while completing a task but later struggle to remember which files have already been inspected. Since traditional file systems do not distinguish between files that have merely been opened and files that have been intentionally reviewed, users frequently reopen documents to verify whether they have already examined them.

**Human Impact**

*   Time lost reopening previously reviewed files.
*   Interrupted workflow.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Duplicate effort.
*   Reduced productivity.

**Context**

Commonly occurs in:

*   Research documentation.
*   Software development.
*   Academic assignments.
*   Business document review.
*   Large project repositories.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is frequently observed when users work through large collections of documents over extended periods. After reviewing numerous files, users often lose track of which documents have already been examined and which remain unread. As a result, they repeatedly reopen files to verify their progress instead of continuing with new work.

**Research Notes**

*   Contributes to **PAT-002 — Memory Dependency.**
*   Focuses on review history rather than file status or recent activity.
*   Related to workflow continuity and information processing.
