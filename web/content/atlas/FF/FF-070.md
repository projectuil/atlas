Users frequently remember the outcome of a previous decision but later struggle to recall the reasoning, assumptions, evidence, or constraints that led to it. Although the final decision remains documented or remembered, the surrounding decision context gradually fades over time. As a result, users often reevaluate previously solved problems because they can no longer understand why the original decision was considered appropriate.

**Human Impact**

*   Repeated analysis.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Reduced confidence in previous decisions.
*   Slower decision-making.
*   Duplicate effort.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Software architecture.
*   Business planning.
*   Research projects.
*   Cybersecurity investigations.
*   Personal long-term planning.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed when users revisit important decisions after weeks, months, or years. Although they recognize the decision itself, they often cannot reconstruct the evidence, assumptions, or trade-offs that justified it. Existing documentation systems typically preserve decision outcomes but provide limited support for preserving the complete reasoning process behind those decisions.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on preserving decision reasoning rather than decision outcomes.
*   Highlights long-term loss of decision context.
*   Related to knowledge continuity, documentation, and organizational memory.
