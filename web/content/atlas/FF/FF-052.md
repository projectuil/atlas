Before beginning a new task, users frequently fail to recognize which knowledge, skills, or information they lack. They often assume they are adequately prepared until they encounter unexpected difficulties during execution. As a result, work is interrupted while users search for missing information, learn prerequisite concepts, or revise their original approach. The challenge lies not in acquiring knowledge itself but in identifying unknown knowledge requirements before work begins.

**Human Impact**

*   Interrupted workflow.
*   Increased learning time.
*   Higher cognitive load.
*   Repeated task interruptions.
*   Reduced preparation efficiency.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Learning new technologies.
*   Software development.
*   Cybersecurity investigations.
*   Academic assignments.
*   Professional training.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is frequently observed when users begin unfamiliar tasks or enter new domains of knowledge. Although users may possess substantial existing knowledge, they often discover important prerequisites only after encountering obstacles during execution. Existing digital learning and productivity systems provide extensive information resources but offer limited support for helping users identify knowledge gaps before beginning a task.

**Research Notes**

*   Builds upon FF-051 by focusing on unknown knowledge rather than knowledge sufficiency.
*   Highlights hidden prerequisite knowledge.
*   Related to learning readiness, preparation, and knowledge discovery.
