Users frequently encounter situations where available evidence is incomplete, conflicting, or inconclusive. Although a decision must still be made, users struggle to maintain confidence because they cannot determine whether the uncertainty is acceptable or whether additional evidence is required. This often results in hesitation, repeated evaluation, or reluctance to commit to a decision.

**Human Impact**

*   Reduced confidence.
*   Delayed decision-making.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Repeated evaluation of evidence.
*   Decision fatigue.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Cybersecurity investigations.
*   Medical diagnosis.
*   Business strategy.
*   Scientific research.
*   Risk management.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed in situations where perfect certainty is unattainable. Users often understand the available evidence but remain uncomfortable making decisions because unresolved uncertainty persists. Existing decision-support systems present evidence but provide limited support for helping users assess whether remaining uncertainty is acceptable for action.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on maintaining confidence under uncertainty.
*   Distinguishes evidence uncertainty from knowledge insufficiency.
*   Related to decision confidence, risk assessment, and uncertainty management.
