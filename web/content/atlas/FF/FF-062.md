Users frequently recognize that their knowledge is incomplete but struggle to estimate how this uncertainty might affect the outcome of their decisions. They often cannot determine whether the missing information is insignificant or whether it could substantially change the final result. This uncertainty makes it difficult to balance the risks of acting immediately against the risks of delaying action to gather additional knowledge.

**Human Impact**

*   Delayed decision-making.
*   Increased uncertainty.
*   Higher cognitive load.
*   Reduced confidence.
*   Greater risk of poor decisions.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Cybersecurity incident response.
*   Business strategy.
*   Medical decision-making.
*   Financial planning.
*   Project management.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed when users must make decisions before obtaining complete information. While users often recognize the existence of knowledge gaps, they struggle to evaluate the practical consequences of those gaps. Existing decision-support systems provide access to information but offer limited support for estimating how incomplete knowledge influences decision outcomes.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on evaluating decision risk caused by missing knowledge.
*   Distinguishes uncertainty from knowledge acquisition.
*   Related to risk assessment, uncertainty, and decision quality.
