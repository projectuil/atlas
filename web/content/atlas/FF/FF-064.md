Users frequently continue researching even after collecting sufficient information because they cannot determine whether additional research will meaningfully improve their decision. They remain uncertain whether important evidence has been overlooked or whether further investigation will simply produce diminishing returns. As a result, users often delay decisions by repeatedly gathering information instead of acting on their current understanding.

**Human Impact**

*   Delayed decision-making.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Decision fatigue.
*   Reduced productivity.
*   Time lost on unnecessary research.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Academic research.
*   Product purchasing.
*   Business analysis.
*   Cybersecurity investigations.
*   Career planning.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed when users perform open-ended research without a clearly defined stopping point. Although users progressively improve their understanding, they often struggle to identify the moment when additional information no longer provides meaningful value. Existing information systems efficiently support information discovery but provide limited support for determining when research is sufficiently complete for confident decision-making.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on determining the endpoint of information gathering.
*   Distinguishes research completion from knowledge sufficiency.
*   Related to decision readiness, uncertainty, and information management.
