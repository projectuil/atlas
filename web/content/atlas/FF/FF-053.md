Users frequently encounter large amounts of information when learning, researching, or making decisions. Although much of this information may be relevant to the broader topic, not all of it is equally important for achieving the user's immediate objective. Users often struggle to determine which information is essential and which can be safely ignored, resulting in unnecessary reading, increased cognitive effort, and difficulty focusing on the knowledge that truly matters.

**Human Impact**

*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Longer decision-making time.
*   Information overload.
*   Reduced learning efficiency.
*   Difficulty maintaining focus.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Academic research.
*   Internet searches.
*   Cybersecurity investigations.
*   Business analysis.
*   Technical documentation.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed when users work with large collections of information from multiple sources. Users frequently consume significantly more information than necessary because they cannot confidently determine which content directly contributes to their objective. Existing information systems provide powerful search capabilities but offer limited support for helping users distinguish essential knowledge from supporting or less relevant information.

**Research Notes**

*   Builds upon FF-051 and FF-052 by focusing on information prioritization.
*   Distinguishes information importance rather than information availability.
*   Related to cognitive load, learning efficiency, and decision quality.
