Users frequently communicate the outcomes of decisions without fully explaining the evidence, assumptions, alternatives, or reasoning that led to those decisions. While the final decision may be documented, the supporting rationale often remains incomplete or omitted. As a result, other individuals struggle to understand, evaluate, or confidently build upon previous decisions.

**Human Impact**

*   Reduced knowledge transfer.
*   Misinterpretation of decisions.
*   Increased communication effort.
*   Repeated clarification.
*   Reduced collaboration efficiency.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Business meetings.
*   Software architecture decisions.
*   Research documentation.
*   Cybersecurity investigations.
*   Government and organizational policy.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed in collaborative environments where decisions are shared among multiple stakeholders. Users often record what was decided but fail to preserve why it was decided. Existing documentation systems effectively capture decision outcomes but provide limited support for preserving the complete reasoning process that supports future understanding.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on communicating decision rationale rather than decision outcomes.
*   Highlights preservation of reasoning for future understanding.
*   Related to collaboration, documentation, and organizational knowledge.
