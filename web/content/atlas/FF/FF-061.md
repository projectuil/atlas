Users frequently understand complex concepts well enough to use them but struggle to explain those concepts in language that others can easily understand. They often rely on technical terminology, omit important intermediate reasoning, or assume prior knowledge that their audience does not possess. As a result, effective knowledge transfer becomes difficult despite the user's own understanding of the subject.

**Human Impact**

*   Ineffective communication.
*   Reduced knowledge sharing.
*   Increased misunderstanding.
*   Higher cognitive effort.
*   Slower learning for others.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs in:**

*   Education.
*   Workplace training.
*   Technical documentation.
*   Software development.
*   Public presentations.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed when individuals attempt to teach, mentor, or document their expertise. Although users may possess deep subject knowledge, they often struggle to simplify explanations without losing essential meaning. Existing communication tools primarily support content creation but provide limited assistance in adapting explanations to different levels of audience understanding.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on communicating knowledge rather than acquiring it.
*   Highlights the gap between understanding and explanation.
*   Related to knowledge transfer, education, and communication.
