Strictly coded form fields auto-reject legitimate inputs due to syntax quirks (e.g., international phone number prefixes, special characters in names, non-standard address lines) without forgiving auto-formatting.

**Human Impact**

*   Direct frustration for users with non-standard names, addresses, or phone formats.
*   Inability to complete necessary forms without altering real personal data.
*   Alienation caused by rigid systems that fail to recognize valid real-world data.

**Context**

Commonly occurs in:

*   International registration forms with rigid localized input masks.
*   Name fields rejecting hyphens, apostrophes, or non-Latin script characters.
*   Postal/zip code inputs enforcing fixed character counts.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users with hyphenated surnames or non-standard addresses encounter immediate inline errors ("Invalid character") forcing them to alter their actual name or address to proceed.

**Research Notes**

*   Contributes to PAT-002 — Authentication Fatigue.
*   Highlights the failure of frontend forms to implement robust input masking and flexible sanitization.
