Arbitrary and complex password requirements (min length, symbols, numbers, upper/lower cases) force users to invent artificial combinations that are hard to remember and difficult to manage without password managers.

**Human Impact**

*   Memory overload and cognitive strain.
*   Tendency to reuse base passwords with minor modifications (insecure habits).
*   Frustration during account setup and subsequent sign-ins.
*   Anxiety over potential lockout.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Registering for new online accounts.
*   Updating expired passwords per enterprise policies.
*   Interacting with legacy security systems.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users frequently write passwords down physically, reuse existing compromised strings, or rely on repeated password reset loops rather than committing new complex rules to memory.

**Research Notes**

*   Contributes to PAT-002 — Authentication Fatigue.
*   Demonstrates how strict security rules can paradoxically lower actual security through human coping mechanisms.
