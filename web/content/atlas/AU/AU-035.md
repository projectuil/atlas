Users receiving unprompted authentication requests (e.g., push notifications, OTP codes) struggle to distinguish real system checks from adversary-driven "MFA fatigue" attacks or social engineering impersonations.

**Human Impact**

*   Account compromise resulting from approving unauthorized push prompts.
*   Persistent worry over receiving unexpected verification messages.
*   Vulnerability to malicious actors posing as support staff requesting OTPs.

**Context**

Commonly occurs via:

*   Unexpected push notifications sent late at night (MFA bombing).
*   Fraudulent support calls asking for "the 6-digit code sent to your phone."
*   Deceptive popup windows embedded on third-party sites.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users frequently approve unprompted push notifications simply to clear the pop-up from their phone screen, succumbing to operational exhaustion rather than assessing threat validity.

**Research Notes**

*   Contributes to PAT-002 — Authentication Fatigue.
*   Illustrates how security prompts without contextual data (location, IP, active app name) fail to protect users.
