As the average user's digital footprint grows to encompass dozens or hundreds of accounts, mandatory identity verification steps, MFA challenges, and re-login policies accumulate. Over time, users adapt by developing predictable, highly repetitive copings (reusing base patterns, reflexively clicking push approvals without reading, auto-saving credentials insecurely) that degrade overall security hygiene and cause continuous digital strain.

**Human Impact**

*   Persistent mental exhaustion when accessing web services.
*   Blind reflex clicks on security warnings and push notifications ("MFA fatigue").
*   Widespread drop in personal security posture due to coping mechanisms.
*   Growing hostility toward platform security prompts.

**Context**

Commonly occurs across:

*   Daily professional digital workflows requiring multiple SSO logins.
*   Cumulative management of consumer accounts (banking, healthcare, shopping, entertainment).
*   Personal identity ecosystems managed across mobile and desktop environments.

**Evidence / Observation**

Observed as a long-term behavioral transformation across all digital demographics. While isolated friction points (such as entering an OTP or completing a captcha) seem manageable individually, their compounding occurrence throughout a single day leads to observable security fatigue, reduced vigilance, and widespread automated coping habits.
