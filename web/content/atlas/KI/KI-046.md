Most computer users hit an "intermediate performance plateau." Moving beyond basic typing and simple shortcuts requires deliberate practice and temporary speed drops, which discourages users from adopting expert keyboard-driven techniques.

**Human Impact**

*   Long-term stagnation at sub-optimal productivity levels.
*   Intimidation by advanced keyboard-centric tools (e.g., Vim, Emacs, tiling window managers).
*   Perceived difficulty hurdle blocking expert skill mastery.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Attempting to learn touch-typing after years of hunt-and-peck usage.
*   Adopting keyboard-only workflows in professional software setups.

**Evidence / Observation**

Longitudinal productivity tracking reveals that users rarely adopt new keyboard habits spontaneously once a baseline "good enough" speed is reached.

**Research Notes**

*   Analyzes the "intermediate plateau" in human-computer interaction skill acquisition.
