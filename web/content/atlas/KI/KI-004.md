A large portion of digital technology users have not mastered touch typing and rely on visual search ("hunt-and-peck") to locate specific keys. Even among experienced computer users, spatial recall for lesser-used character keys remains weak, requiring users to visually scan the physical keyboard repeatedly during input.

**Human Impact**

*   Slower text composition and reduced typing speed.
*   Neck strain and visual fatigue caused by continuous movement between screen and keyboard.
*   Cognitive fragmentation as attention shifts away from thought composition to mechanical key location.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Entering long-form prose, technical documents, or software code.
*   Typing in low-light conditions on non-backlit keyboards.
*   Switching between different laptop keyboards with varied key placement.

**Evidence / Observation**

Eyetracking and video studies reveal that non-touch typists divert their eyes to the keyboard up to several times per sentence. This constant repositioning of visual focus leads to higher error rates and increased task completion times.

**Research Notes**

*   Highlights the gap between universal digital reliance and physical interface mastery.
*   Establishes base-level physical interaction friction.
