Applications rarely teach shortcuts interactively. Standard discoverability methods (e.g., passive menu tooltips) fail to build motor memory, leaving users unaware of time-saving keyboard workflows during daily tasks.

**Human Impact**

*   Users remain stuck in slow, entry-level interaction patterns indefinitely.
*   Lack of awareness regarding efficiency gains offered by keyboard controls.
*   Absence of contextual feedback encouraging shortcut adoption.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Onboarding into complex web or desktop applications.
*   Performing repetitive mouse tasks that have simple single-key equivalents.

**Evidence / Observation**

A/B testing shows that passive menu labels yield less than 3% shortcut adoption, whereas contextual "did you know?" micro-prompts or shortcut reminders increase adoption significantly.

**Research Notes**

*   Explores in-context pedagogical interface design for motor skill acquisition.
