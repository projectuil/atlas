During routine computer tasks, users continuously remove their hand from the keyboard to grab the mouse, move the pointer to click a field or button, and then reposition their hand back on the home row. Because users are unaware of simple keyboard navigation equivalents (such as Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, Space, or directional hotkeys), this constant physical switching slows down performance and increases ergonomic fatigue.

**Human Impact**

*   Accumulation of micro-delays that substantially slow down form filling and document editing.
*   Increased physical strain on the arm, wrist, and shoulder from continuous hand transitions.
*   Fragmented focus during data entry activities.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Filling out multi-field web forms or spreadsheets.
*   Navigating modal dialogs and confirmation popups.
*   Browsing search results and selecting links.

**Evidence / Observation**

Time-motion studies in human factors engineering consistently demonstrate that switching between keyboard and pointing device requires approximately 0.4 to 1.2 seconds per transition. Accumulating hundreds of these switches daily adds significant time waste to computer tasks.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on physical input modality switching cost.
*   Crucial for accessibility and power-user interface optimizations.
