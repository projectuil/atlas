Modifier keys (Control, Option/Alt, Command/Super, Shift, Fn) do not output visible characters on their own; instead, they alter the signal sent by other keys. Non-technical users struggle to construct a clear mental model of how these keys interact with standard keys, often confusing Ctrl with Alt or struggling to understand why Fn is required for media functions.

**Human Impact**

*   Confusion when following instructions that require modifier key combinations.
*   Incorrect execution of multi-key shortcuts.
*   Hesitation when encountering cross-platform terminology (e.g., Cmd vs Ctrl, Option vs Alt).

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Switching between Windows, macOS, and Linux hardware.
*   Attempting to adjust screen brightness, volume, or wireless settings using Fn function rows.
*   Following technical guides or software tutorials.

**Evidence / Observation**

Beginner and intermediate computer literacy tests reveal high rates of error when users are asked to perform operations involving modifier keys, particularly when distinguishing between Left Ctrl, Right Alt, or Fn combinations.

**Research Notes**

*   Identifies cognitive abstraction friction inherent in multi-layer physical hardware.
*   Foundation for cross-platform input mapping standardization.
