Typing accurately while keeping sight fixed on a primary source (such as a physical paper document, an external screen, or a video conference speaker) requires strong tactile spatial awareness. Because modern keyboards often feature flat key profiles, minimal key travel, or subtle homing bumps (F and J), users quickly lose their hand orientation when looking away.

**Human Impact**

*   Accumulation of unseen typing errors when transcribing or taking notes during meetings.
*   Forced interruptions to look down and re-center hands on the physical home row.
*   Decreased speed and confidence during eyes-free data entry.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Transcribing text from physical notes or secondary monitors.
*   Taking notes while maintaining eye contact during video meetings.
*   Typing on flat chiclet or touch-based virtual keyboards.

**Evidence / Observation**

Studies measuring touch-typist accuracy show significant drop-offs in performance when tactile homing indicators (F/J bumps) are degraded or missing, demonstrating high reliance on subtle tactile feedback for eyes-free input.

**Research Notes**

*   Explores tactile hardware feedback and spatial anchoring.
*   Vital for physical keyboard hardware evaluation and virtual keyboard design.
