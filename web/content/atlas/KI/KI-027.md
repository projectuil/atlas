Switching between keyboard layouts (e.g., QWERTY, AZERTY, Dvorak, Colemak, or regional variations) invalidates established motor memory. Users experience high error rates when key locations for letters, punctuation, or numbers shift.

**Human Impact**

*   Sharp drop in typing speed and immediate increase in error rate.
*   Severe mental fatigue caused by conscious monitoring of finger movements.
*   Frustration during international travel or shared workstation usage.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Bilingual workers switch software layouts on identical physical hardware.
*   Cross-border employees use localized physical laptops.

**Evidence / Observation**

Typists switching layouts show up to a 70% decrease in WPM (words per minute) during early adaptation phases, with frequent keystroke reversals on shifted keys (e.g., A and Q).

**Research Notes**

*   Explores motor interference in spatial keyboard mapping.
*   Highlights the benefit of software-level visual key overlays.
