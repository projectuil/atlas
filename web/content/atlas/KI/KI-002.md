Standard physical computer keyboards contain legacy keys (e.g., SysRq, Scroll Lock, Pause/Break, Insert, Num Lock) and legacy layout choices inherited from early typewriters and terminal terminals. Everyday computer users rarely understand the purpose or function of these specialized keys, leading to confusion, avoidance, or accidental key presses that cause unexpected system behaviors.

**Human Impact**

*   Hesitation when interacting with unfamiliar regions of the physical keyboard.
*   Accidental triggering of modal states (e.g., overwriting text via Insert).
*   Confusion regarding unused hardware capabilities.
*   Higher cognitive friction for novice and non-technical computer users.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Using full-size desktop keyboards with dedicated navigation/function clusters.
*   Accidental keypresses occurring near backspace or arrow keys.
*   Working with legacy terminal emulators or remote desktop setups.

**Evidence / Observation**

In general computing environments, keys like Scroll Lock and Pause/Break remain untouched by a vast majority of users. Furthermore, keypress tracking indicates that accidental presses of Insert regularly frustrate users who suddenly begin overwriting text without realizing why the input mode altered.

**Research Notes**

*   Highlights the legacy hardware debt inherent in modern computing interfaces.
*   Forms a foundation for research into streamlined and minimal physical keyboard layouts.
