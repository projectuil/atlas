In-grained, inefficient keyboard habits (e.g., pressing Backspace 20 times instead of Ctrl+Backspace, using mouse to highlight text instead of Shift+Arrows) are hard to break because deep motor neural pathways default to familiar routines under time pressure.

**Human Impact**

*   Persistence of slow, high-effort input mechanics despite knowing faster methods.
*   Relapse into old typing habits under stress or deadline pressure.
*   Frustration with inability to override muscle memory.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Editing long text blocks using repetitive backspacing.
*   Re-learning cursor positioning techniques in text editors.

**Evidence / Observation**

Observations show that even after learning word-deletion shortcuts, users under high cognitive load revert instantly to rapid repeated single-character backspacing.

**Research Notes**

*   Examines motor habit suppression and unlearning friction.
