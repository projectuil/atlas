Certain software applications require multi-key "chords" involving three or four keys simultaneously (e.g., Ctrl+Shift+Alt+V or Cmd+Option+Shift+S). Coordinating multiple fingers across widely spaced physical keys causes physical awkwardness, finger contortion ("finger gymnastics"), and frequent execution failure due to releasing one key prematurely.

**Human Impact**

*   Physical strain and discomfort in hands and fingers.
*   High rate of failed shortcut triggers due to timing or release errors.
*   Frustration with poorly designed software keybindings.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Executing advanced commands in graphics software (e.g., Photoshop, Illustrator).
*   Using developer tools, text editors, or IDEs (e.g., VS Code, Emacs).
*   Managing complex window management or screen capture commands.

**Evidence / Observation**

Ergonomic assessments indicate that 4-key physical chords exceed comfortable finger spans for a significant percentage of users, often forcing the use of two hands to execute a single shortcut command.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on physical motor constraints during input interaction.
*   Recommends sticky keys, sequential key chording, or command palettes over complex physical chords.
