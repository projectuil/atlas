While users usually remember the spatial location of primary alphanumeric keys (A–Z, 0–9), they struggle to quickly locate special symbols and punctuation marks (e.g., {, }, \[, \], ~, |, \\, #, @). Because these characters are used less frequently in general prose, spatial muscle memory for them is considerably weaker.

**Human Impact**

*   Sudden drops in typing speed when inputting code, math formulas, or passwords.
*   Physical scanning of keycaps to find required symbols.
*   Increased error rates due to pressing adjacent symbol keys or incorrect Shift combinations.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Writing programming code or shell scripts.
*   Inputting complex passwords containing special characters.
*   Formatting structured text markup (Markdown, HTML, JSON).

**Evidence / Observation**

Keylog and movement studies demonstrate significant pauses prior to pressing symbol keys among both intermediate typists and programmers, as users visually locate the target keycap or mentally recalculate the required modifier combination.

**Research Notes**

*   Highlights specific ergonomic limitations for specialized technical workflows.
*   Closely tied to code editor auto-completion research.
