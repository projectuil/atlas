As typing speed increases, spatial accuracy naturally decreases. Users regularly clip adjacent physical keys, hit Caps Lock instead of A or Tab, or press Enter prematurely. Small variations in key switch travel, actuation force, or keycap spacing across different keyboards exacerbate these physical input errors.

**Human Impact**

*   Interrupted momentum while typing.
*   Frustration caused by unintentional execution of actions (e.g., submitting forms prematurely via Enter).
*   Need for constant visual monitoring of text output.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Typing at high speeds under time pressure.
*   Working on compact laptop keyboards with narrow key spacing.
*   Transitioning between mechanical and membrane keyboards.

**Evidence / Observation**

Keystroke dynamics data shows that error rate correlates exponentially with typing speed above certain personal thresholds. Mispresses of adjacent key pairs (e.g., N vs M, E vs R) account for the vast majority of mechanical typing mistakes.

**Research Notes**

*   Connects hardware physical key design (key size, key distance) with human motor limits.
*   Informs input filtering algorithms and keyboard switch design.
