When confronting a massive, monolithic topic (e.g., "Learn Machine Learning" or "Master World History"), novice learners lack the domain expertise required to slice the monolith into sequential, bite-sized chunks. Without proper "chunking," the cognitive load of looking at the entire subject causes immediate overwhelm.

**Human Impact**

*   Intimidation preventing the learner from even starting.
*   Taking on chunks that are far too large to digest in a single session.
*   Disorganized study sessions that jump erratically around the subject matter.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Beginning entirely self-directed study without a formal syllabus.
*   Trying to learn from macro-resources like documentation hubs or wikis.

**Evidence / Observation**

Learners sit down intending to "learn JavaScript" on a Saturday, open a 500-page manual, become instantly overwhelmed by the Table of Contents, and close the book 10 minutes later.

**Research Notes**

Highlights the critical psychological importance of automated syllabus generation and micro-learning modules.
