Skill acquisition requires intense, focused bandwidth. When a learner pivots focus to a new, difficult skill (e.g., learning a new programming language or a new physical sport), the time and cognitive resources previously spent maintaining their older skills vanish. This results in rapid atrophy of foundational or secondary skills.

**Human Impact**

*   Frustrating "two steps forward, one step back" progression.
*   Anxiety regarding the loss of hard-earned abilities.
*   Avoidance of new learning out of fear it will overwrite existing competence.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Polyglots attempting to learn a third or fourth language.
*   Professionals rotating into management roles, losing their technical hard skills.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users who switch to learning Python for six months frequently find they have forgotten essential CSS/HTML syntax they previously knew natively, forcing them to re-read beginner documentation.

**Research Notes**

Focuses on the cognitive mechanics of interference theory and the necessity of automated maintenance-repetition schedules.
