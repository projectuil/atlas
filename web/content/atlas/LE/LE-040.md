Human motivation is designed for short-term reward cycles. When pursuing a subject that requires years to master (like a foreign language or advanced mathematics), the absence of immediate, tangible milestones causes dopamine systems to crash. Consistency evaporates as the gap between the current state and the end goal feels hopelessly vast.

**Human Impact**

*   High attrition rates around the 3-week to 3-month mark of new endeavors.
*   Binge-and-purge study cycles (cramming followed by weeks of avoidance).
*   Deep guilt surrounding abandoned projects and goals.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Self-studying for major multi-year exams like the Bar or CPA.
*   Undertaking complete career transformations while working full-time.

**Evidence / Observation**

Gyms and language apps see a 70%+ drop-off in February after New Year's resolutions fail, entirely due to the friction of long-term horizon goals lacking short-term localized rewards.

**Research Notes**

Focuses on gamification, habit formation, and the necessity of micro-credentialing in ed-tech.
