Learning systems are fundamentally biased toward "forward momentum"—unlocking the next module, buying the next book, reaching the next level. However, actual mastery often requires pausing forward progress to solidify and revise existing, shaky foundations. Learners feel constant friction trying to decide if they should press forward into the unknown or retreat to fix the cracks.

**Human Impact**

*   FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) regarding new, exciting topics.
*   Building advanced skills on top of weak fundamentals, leading to eventual total collapse.
*   Guilt when dedicating study hours to things they "should already know."

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Nearing the end of a semester before finals.
*   Experiencing recurring bugs or errors in advanced projects stemming from beginner mistakes.

**Evidence / Observation**

Learners consistently choose to watch a tutorial on a brand-new, cutting-edge framework rather than spending that hour finally memorizing the core foundational algorithms they continuously mess up.

**Research Notes**

Highlights the need for diagnostic pacing systems that temporarily lock forward progress until foundational decay is repaired.
