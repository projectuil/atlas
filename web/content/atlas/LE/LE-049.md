Modern self-education is highly fractured. A learner might stitch together a curriculum using a Udemy course, three YouTube channels, a textbook, and ChatGPT. Because these sources use different taxonomies, visual metaphors, and pacing, the learner spends massive cognitive energy translating between them to form a single, coherent mental model.

**Human Impact**

*   Extreme cognitive fatigue from translating domain terminology across different instructors.
*   Inadvertent creation of conflicting rulesets in the brain.
*   Wasted time reorganizing notes to force alignment between disparate sources.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Bootstrapping an education for free using open internet resources.
*   Researching complex, highly debated topics (dietary science, economics).

**Evidence / Observation**

A learner realizes after two weeks of confusion that "Source A's Vector," "Source B's Array," and "Source C's List" were all referring to the exact same fundamental concept, just using different colloquial framing.

**Research Notes**

Focuses on the friction of unstandardized pedagogical taxonomies across the creator economy.
