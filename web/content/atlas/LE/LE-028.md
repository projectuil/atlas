Modern digital learning provides an overabundance of choices. When a learner finishes a foundational module, they are often presented with several divergent branches (e.g., front-end vs. back-end, theory vs. application). Without guided pathway constraints, learners suffer from analysis paralysis and lose momentum.

**Human Impact**

*   Decision fatigue leading to prolonged periods of inaction.
*   "Tutorial hell" where users repeatedly start foundational courses in different branches rather than advancing.
*   Anxiety over choosing the "wrong" path and wasting time.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Using open-ended platforms like YouTube, Coursera, or Udemy for self-guided learning.
*   Reaching the intermediate plateau where subjects naturally become highly specialized.

**Evidence / Observation**

Data shows learners frequently spend more time building complex Notion boards or spreadsheets to map out what they _should_ learn next rather than actually executing the learning.

**Research Notes**

Points to a critical failure in open-ended learning environments where abundance of choice degrades actual knowledge acquisition.
