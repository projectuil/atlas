Learning journeys stretching across months or years create temporal gaps between related concepts. A learner may master Concept A in Month 1, but fail to recognize its application to Concept Z in Month 8, because the brain has siloed the knowledge temporally rather than conceptually.

**Human Impact**

*   Inability to synthesize comprehensive solutions to macro-problems.
*   Frustration when "knowing" all the pieces but failing to see the complete puzzle.
*   Frequent need to re-review old modules to establish missing links.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Undertaking multi-year degree programs or comprehensive bootcamps.
*   Working on capstone projects that require full-stack integration.

**Evidence / Observation**

During capstone exams, students successfully recall individual formulas but fail completely when a single question requires bridging a concept from Chapter 2 with a concept from Chapter 15.

**Research Notes**

Underlines the necessity of spaced repetition and interleaved practice in longitudinal curriculum design.
