Traditional education chops reality into discrete silos: Math is at 9 AM, Physics at 10 AM, and History at 11 AM. Because topics are presented with hard borders, learners fail to recognize that the math formulas are identical to the physics models, or that the historical economic crash explains the literature of the era.

**Human Impact**

*   Inefficient, redundant learning (learning the same underlying logic twice in two different contexts).
*   Inability to think laterally or invent creative solutions across disciplines.
*   Fragile knowledge that only works in the specific "room" it was learned in.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Moving through rigid, modularized curriculums (high school, corporate training).
*   Using flashcard systems that randomize concepts without maintaining relational webs.

**Evidence / Observation**

Students frequently express utter shock when a professor in a computer science class uses a biology concept (like neural networks or genetic algorithms), treating it as an illegal crossover rather than a natural relationship.

**Research Notes**

Validates the rise of interconnected note-taking tools (Obsidian, Roam) that map knowledge as a graph rather than a linear hierarchy.
