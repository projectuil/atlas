Learners often gravitate toward whichever domain is more comfortable. Some obsess over reading theory but never build anything, developing "analysis paralysis." Others rush straight into blind execution without reading instructions, developing terrible habits. Managing the ideal pendulum swing between studying rules and practicing execution is exceptionally difficult without a coach.

**Human Impact**

*   "Book smart" learners who completely freeze when handed a real-world tool.
*   "Street smart" learners who hit hard skill ceilings because they don't understand the underlying theory.
*   Deep frustration when transition between the two modes reveals profound incompetence.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Self-teaching creative disciplines (music production, graphic design, writing).
*   Navigating trade skills or complex software engineering.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users watch 40 hours of masterclasses on digital painting but stare blankly at an empty canvas, terrified to make a mark because practical muscle memory was never developed alongside theoretical vision.

**Research Notes**

Identifies the necessity for continuous, interleaved theory/practice loops in educational software.
