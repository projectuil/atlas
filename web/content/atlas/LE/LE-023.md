.


Initial rapid progress gives way to diminishing returns at advanced levels, where mastering subtle concepts requires far higher effort per incremental skill gain.

**Human Impact**

*   Disillusionment as dopamine hits from quick wins vanish.
*   Frustration with the steep effort-to-reward ratio.
*   Giving up right before reaching expert-level competency.

**Context**

Commonly occurs during:

*   The "intermediate plateau" in technical mastery or language learning.
*   Moving from beginner mastery to professional-grade problem-solving.

**Evidence / Observation**

Student drop-off curves show an initial plateau drop-off where effort demands increase exponentially for marginal skill gains.

**Research Notes**

*   Demonstrates the necessity of shifting extrinsic reward mechanics to intrinsic goal alignment at advanced stages.
