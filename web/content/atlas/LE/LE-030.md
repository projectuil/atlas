Many subjects introduce a simplified mental model of a concept early on (e.g., Bohr model of the atom), only to completely dismantle and replace it with a highly complex model later (e.g., quantum mechanics). Learners struggle with the friction of unlearning the simplified rule set and accepting the new, deeper complexity.

**Human Impact**

*   Feelings of betrayal or confusion when early lessons are revealed to be "technically incorrect."
*   Friction in adopting new paradigms due to rigid adherence to early, simplified models.
*   Confusion when transitioning from introductory to advanced resources.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Advancing from 101-level survey courses to 301-level specialist courses.
*   Moving from high-level abstractions to low-level implementation details in technology.

**Evidence / Observation**

Learners actively resist adopting complex models, frequently asking, "Why can't we just use the first rule we learned?" and requiring extensive un-learning exercises before progress resumes.

**Research Notes**

Focuses on the pedagogical challenge of "constructive scaffolding" versus "destructive scaffolding."
