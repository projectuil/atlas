Human memory does not feature a native "version control" system. Once a paradigm is deeply learned, users assume it remains static. In rapidly shifting fields, learners unknowingly apply deprecated methodologies, unaware that the industry consensus or underlying technology shifted years ago.

**Human Impact**

*   Professional embarrassment or critical failure when applying obsolete knowledge.
*   Stubborn resistance to new paradigms out of reliance on outdated foundational truths.
*   Wasted effort solving problems that modern tools handle automatically.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Returning to a technical field (software, medicine, SEO) after a multi-year hiatus.
*   Relying on old textbooks or professors who haven't practiced in the modern industry.

**Evidence / Observation**

Developers continuously write dozens of lines of manual boiler-plate code to solve a problem that was natively solved by a single core function added to the language update three years prior.

**Research Notes**

Highlights the need for continuous professional education systems to map delta-updates to existing mental models.
