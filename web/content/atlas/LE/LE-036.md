When a learner fails a test or cannot execute a skill, the root cause is rarely singular. It could be lack of sleep, poor foundational knowledge, confusing curriculum, anxiety, or bad study methods. Lacking diagnostic tools, learners usually blame their own intelligence, missing the environmental or systemic factors destroying their progress.

**Human Impact**

*   Destructive self-talk and dropping out of programs prematurely.
*   Attempting to fix the wrong variable (e.g., studying 5 more hours when the issue is actually test anxiety).
*   Repeated cyclical failure across different subjects.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Navigating high-stakes environments like professional certifications or university finals.
*   Learning in chaotic physical environments (loud homes, exhausting jobs).

**Evidence / Observation**

A user consistently fails practice exams and concludes "I'm just bad at math," completely unaware that their failure correlates exactly with the modules where the core textbook author changed.

**Research Notes**

Explores multi-variable attribution in educational psychology and self-regulated learning.
