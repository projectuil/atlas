Gamified platforms and strict grading rubrics incentivize learners to optimize for task completion (getting the green checkmark) rather than deep comprehension. Learners copy-paste solutions, guess multiple-choice answers, or blindly follow step-by-step guides, successfully "completing" the module while acquiring zero structural knowledge.

**Human Impact**

*   The illusion of competence leading to harsh reality checks in real-world scenarios.
*   Near-total knowledge decay within weeks of module completion.
*   Resentment toward the learning process once the superficial metrics are removed.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Using automated coding platforms, gamified language apps, or mandatory corporate compliance training.
*   Studying strictly to pass a test rather than to apply the skill.

**Evidence / Observation**

A user maintains a 300-day streak on a language learning app by guessing context clues in mini-games, but is completely incapable of ordering a coffee in that language in real life.

**Research Notes**

Examines the tension between performance metrics (task completion) and actual pedagogical outcomes (knowledge transfer).
