Not all chapters in a textbook or modules in a course are created equal. Some contain critical foundational concepts that demand weeks of deep focus, while others are trivial side-notes meant to be skimmed. Novices allocate their study time uniformly across all pages, leading to massive inefficiencies and poor mastery of core concepts.

**Human Impact**

*   Running out of study time before reaching the most critical parts of the subject.
*   Experiencing burnout from over-studying easy, low-value sections.
*   Misalignment of effort versus actual assessment weighting.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Cramming for finals with dense, non-hierarchical notes.
*   Following a self-imposed study schedule based purely on page count rather than conceptual weight.

**Evidence / Observation**

Students frequently spend 3 days memorizing the introductory historical context of a textbook chapter, leaving only 3 hours to practice the complex mathematical formulas at the end of the chapter that make up 90% of the exam grade.

**Research Notes**

Points to the need for time-weighted metadata on educational resources (e.g., "Spend 2 hours here, skim this next part in 10 minutes").
