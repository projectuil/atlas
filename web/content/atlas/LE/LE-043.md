A major disconnect exists in many courses where the lecture explains a concept at a 101 level (e.g., "Here is how a for-loop works"), but the immediate practical homework requires a 301 level of synthesis (e.g., "Build an algorithm sorting matrix data"). This massive difficulty spike shatters the learner's scaffolding.

**Human Impact**

*   Feelings of intense frustration and betrayal by the curriculum designer.
*   Total loss of momentum requiring external interventions (tutors, cheating) to bridge the gap.
*   Conclusion that they are "not smart enough" because the lecture felt easy but the homework feels impossible.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   University courses separate lecture professors from lab instructors.
*   Using coding bootcamps where assignments are pulled from disparate real-world problems.

**Evidence / Observation**

The classic "Draw an Owl" meme perfectly encapsulates this friction: Step 1 is drawing two circles (the lecture), and Step 2 is drawing the rest of the highly detailed owl (the homework).

**Research Notes**

Focuses on the pedagogical need for micro-graduated practical exercises that slowly remove training wheels.
