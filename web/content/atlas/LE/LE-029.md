Educational texts and lectures frequently blend core principles with historical anecdotes, edge cases, and advanced footnotes. Novice learners, lacking a mental model of the subject, weigh all inputs equally and expend massive effort attempting to memorize trivial supplementary data alongside critical foundational rules.

**Human Impact**

*   Rapid cognitive overload and early burnout.
*   Poor performance on practical assessments due to focusing on edge cases rather than core rules.
*   Slower overall progression through learning modules.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Reading comprehensive academic textbooks or dense technical documentation.
*   Preparing for standardized tests without a strict syllabus.

**Evidence / Observation**

When reviewing student notes, instructors often find that beginners highlight 80% of the text on a page, failing to isolate the 20% of information that actually drives understanding.

**Research Notes**

Demonstrates the need for intelligent content presentation that visually or structurally separates the "critical path" from "deep dives."
