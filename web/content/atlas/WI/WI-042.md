Web browsers treat pages as isolated visual silos. When performing multi-source research, users lack native browser tools to highlight, clip, aggregate, and structure data extracted across multiple sites. Users must resort to chaotic workarounds—such as pasting unformatted text and links into external documents or maintaining dozens of open tabs.

**Human Impact**

*   Heavy cognitive burden assembling and normalizing fragmented information.
*   Loss of source attribution and context when copying plain text snippets.
*   Fragmented research workflows requiring continuous app-switching.
*   Disorganization leading to incomplete analysis or research errors.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Conducting academic, legal, or market research across multiple portals.
*   Planning family travel, gathering price estimates, and compare choices.
*   Compiling technical documentation and code samples from disparate sites.

**Evidence / Observation**

In field research, users conducting multi-site research routinely keep a scratchpad document open alongside their browser window. They engage in constant copy-paste cycles, often losing track of which source URL yielded a specific fact because standard OS copy commands do not automatically attach underlying metadata or source URLs.

**Research Notes**

*   Highlights the absence of native dynamic synthesis mechanisms in standard browser UIs.
*   Examines the manual overhead of cross-platform information gathering.
*   Essential foundation for Multi-Site Synthesis and Contextual Clipping patterns.
