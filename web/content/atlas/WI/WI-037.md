Websites use HTTP cookies, LocalStorage, and IndexedDB to store state locally on the user's machine—enabling persistent logins, shopping cart retentions, and customized layouts. Because these storage mechanisms operate silently in the background, users struggle to comprehend how web pages "remember" their choices without an explicit account or active server connection.

**Human Impact**

*   Confusion when items remain in shopping carts across guest sessions.
*   Unease over how websites recall location or preferences without explicit sign-in.
*   Inability to troubleshoot corrupted site states caused by stale local storage data.
*   Reliance on inaccurate mental models regarding web tracking.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Visiting e-commerce platforms as a guest user after closing the browser.
*   Noticing customized display preferences on sites never explicitly registered for.
*   Experiencing visual glitches on web apps that retain outdated cached client data.

**Evidence / Observation**

When non-technical users are asked how an unauthenticated website remembered their site filter settings from three days prior, they frequently speculate that the website "recognized their computer screen" or "scanned their hardware." The technical reality of local client cookies remains opaque, leading to widespread confusion when clearing cookies wipes those convenient states.

**Research Notes**

*   Details non-technical mental models surrounding client-side state preservation.
*   Bridges basic web architecture with user perception of site persistence.
*   Informs State Mental Model and Invisible Storage patterns.
