As the average user accumulates hundreds of registered web accounts over time, managing unique credentials, security parameters, linked emails, and active subscriptions becomes overwhelming. Without centralized management tools, users default to insecure practices (e.g., password reuse) or lose track of active accounts entirely.

**Human Impact**

*   High cognitive fatigue managing an expanding digital account footprint.
*   Severe security vulnerabilities caused by universal password reuse.
*   Financial loss from forgotten, auto-renewing web subscriptions.
*   Inability to systematically audit or delete abandoned online profiles.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Attempting an audit of active paid subscriptions or account permissions.
*   Resetting compromised credentials across multiple platforms following a breach notice.
*   Transitioning primary email addresses or phone numbers across external web services.

**Evidence / Observation**

Research demonstrates that users frequently maintain upwards of 100+ web accounts, yet rely on fewer than 3 distinct password variants across all of them. When notified of a data breach on one site, users struggle to list which other platforms share those compromised credentials, leading to account takeover chains.

**Research Notes**

*   Documents the crisis of non-centralized account sprawl across the unbundled web.
*   Direct driver of security vulnerabilities and password fatigue.
*   Connects to broad identity decay patterns (WI-050).
