Over years of web usage, an individual's identity is continuously fragmented and embedded across hundreds of independent services via OAuth SSO tokens, federated sign-ins, API integrations, and shared data brokers. Users completely lose structural awareness of where their data resides, which apps retain active access tokens, and how their identity nodes connect across the broader web ecosystem.

**Human Impact**

*   Total loss of agency and oversight regarding personal digital identity boundaries.
*   Cascade security vulnerabilities: a single compromised core account invalidates dozens of linked web tools.
*   Inability to systematically audit, revoke, or clean up third-party app permissions.
*   Paralysis and alienation when attempting comprehensive privacy management.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Using "Sign in with Google/Apple" across dozens of secondary apps and websites over several years.
*   Linking cloud storage, payment, and social accounts to third-party web utilities.
*   Attempting a complete digital audit or decommissioning a primary identity node (e.g., closing a primary email account).

**Evidence / Observation**

In observational audits of long-term web users, participants asked to list all third-party services with active access to their primary account typically underestimate the count by 80%. When shown their actual OAuth management dashboard—revealing dozens of forgotten applications retaining continuous read/write permissions to their data—users express disbelief and anxiety.
