When access credentials are lost, users must navigate account recovery pathways that rely on secondary channels (e.g., recovery emails, SMS OTPs, multi-factor authenticator apps, security questions). When these recovery channels are out of date, misconfigured, or inaccessible, the recovery process breaks down, permanently locking users out of their web accounts.

**Human Impact**

*   High anxiety over permanent loss of personal data, documents, or purchase history.
*   Time wasted completing multi-step verification challenges.
*   Total abandonment of web platforms when recovery flows fail.
*   Support system strain from manual identity verification requests.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Attempting to log into an account after changing phone numbers or email providers.
*   Accessing services that require physical 2FA hardware keys or authenticator apps that were lost.
*   Attempting recovery on legacy accounts configured with forgotten security questions.

**Evidence / Observation**

During usability testing, standard password reset links frequently fail because users no longer hold access to the original secondary email address provided years prior. When faced with fallback identity verification steps (such as legacy security questions like "First pet's name"), users express frustration over precise casing, spelling variations, or completely forgotten answers, resulting in task abandonment.

**Research Notes**

*   Explores structural breakdown in identity verification fallbacks.
*   Highlights the fragility of multi-factor account dependencies.
*   Key component of long-term digital identity lockouts.
