Because web platforms support diverse registration protocols—ranging from traditional email/password forms to dynamic Social Single Sign-On (SSO) providers (e.g., Google, Apple, Microsoft, GitHub)—users struggle to remember which identity method or email address they originally used for a given site.

**Human Impact**

*   High authentication failure rates and locked account states.
*   Accidental creation of duplicate accounts, resulting in fragmented data.
*   Increased time spent trying multiple SSO buttons sequentially.
*   Psychological fatigue associated with basic site entry.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Logging into e-commerce sites visited only during seasonal sales.
*   Accessing web-based tools that offer multiple federated login options.
*   Returning to enterprise or SaaS platforms after extended leaves.

**Evidence / Observation**

Usability monitoring reveals that users presented with a login screen routinely click through multiple SSO providers sequentially ("Sign in with Google," then "Sign in with Apple," then native email input) until one successfully authenticates. This trial-and-error auth flow frequently results in users accidentally creating a new account under a different provider, isolating them from their prior history.

**Research Notes**

*   Captures identity fragmentation created by competing auth protocols.
*   Emphasizes the mental overhead of maintaining multi-provider identity mapping.
*   Direct contributor to auth failure drop-off metrics.
