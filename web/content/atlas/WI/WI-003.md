Users frequently visit websites knowing exactly what information they need but struggle to locate it because every website organizes content differently. Important information such as pricing, documentation, contact details, downloads, FAQs, or support resources may be placed under different menus, hidden within multiple pages, or presented using unfamiliar terminology. This inconsistency forces users to spend additional time searching within individual websites.

**Human Impact**

*   Increased time locating information.
*   Interrupted workflow.
*   Frustration caused by inconsistent page organization.
*   Increased navigation effort.
*   Reduced productivity during web browsing.

**Context**

**Commonly occurs when:**

*   Searching documentation.
*   Finding contact information.
*   Downloading software or files.
*   Looking for pricing or subscription details.
*   Navigating large organizational websites.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is repeatedly observed across websites of different sizes and industries. Users often move between multiple pages before locating the information they originally intended to find. Many websites organize content according to internal business structures rather than user expectations, requiring visitors to interpret unfamiliar menu labels and navigation hierarchies. Even after successfully locating information on one website, users cannot rely on the same navigation approach when visiting another platform because content organization differs significantly.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on locating information after entering a website.

*   Represents one of the most common navigation-related Human Frictions on the web.
