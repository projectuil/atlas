New computer users often struggle to distinguish between fundamental operating system concepts such as files, folders, storage drives, and applications. Although these elements appear together during everyday computer use, users frequently misunderstand their individual roles and relationships. This confusion makes it difficult to navigate the operating system, organize information, and perform basic computing tasks confidently.

**Human Impact**

*   Difficulty learning basic computer operations.
*   Increased confusion while navigating the operating system.
*   Higher dependence on external assistance.
*   Mistakes when organizing files and applications.
*   Reduced confidence using computers.

**Context**

Commonly occurs:

*   During first-time computer use.
*   In digital literacy programs.
*   Among users transitioning from smartphones.
*   While learning Windows, macOS, or Linux.
*   When organizing personal files.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed among beginner computer users. Questions such as _"Is a folder an application?"_, _"Is the C: drive the same as a folder?"_, or _"Is Microsoft Word a file?"_ frequently arise during introductory computer training. While operating systems visually present these objects together, they rarely explain their distinct purposes, leaving users to develop their understanding through repeated experience and trial and error.
