As operating systems evolve, they introduce new features, interfaces, background services, security mechanisms, configuration options, and automation. While these additions improve functionality, they also increase system complexity. Over time, users develop fragmented knowledge of individual features without forming a coherent understanding of how the operating system functions as an integrated system. This fragmented mental model makes it increasingly difficult to predict system behaviour, troubleshoot problems, and confidently perform unfamiliar tasks.

**Human Impact**

*   Reduced confidence when interacting with unfamiliar system features.
*   Increased dependence on tutorials, internet searches, and technical support.
*   Difficulty troubleshooting problems systematically.
*   Greater cognitive load when learning new operating system features.
*   Hesitation to modify system settings due to fear of unintended consequences.
*   Fragmented understanding that limits long-term digital literacy.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Operating systems introduce new features and interfaces.
*   Users transition to newer operating system versions.
*   Performing advanced system configuration.
*   Troubleshooting unfamiliar problems.
*   Learning operating systems through isolated tasks rather than foundational concepts.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed across desktop operating systems. Users often become proficient at completing routine tasks yet struggle when required to perform unfamiliar operations or diagnose unexpected issues. Rather than relying on an internal understanding of the operating system, they frequently depend on memorized procedures, search engines, or step-by-step tutorials. As operating systems continue to grow in complexity, this gap between system capability and user understanding becomes increasingly pronounced.
