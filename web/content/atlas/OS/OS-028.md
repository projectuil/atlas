As users interact with multiple application windows, important windows frequently become hidden behind others, minimized, moved to another display, or placed outside the user's immediate view. Users often remember that the application is open but struggle to relocate it quickly. This disrupts workflow and leads to unnecessary searching, repeated application launches, or accidental duplication of workspaces.

**Human Impact**

*   Time lost locating hidden windows.
*   Interrupted workflow.
*   Duplicate application windows opened unnecessarily.
*   Increased frustration during multitasking.
*   Reduced awareness of the current desktop workspace.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Working with many overlapping windows.
*   Using multiple monitors.
*   Frequently minimizing applications.
*   Switching between virtual desktops.
*   Returning to previously opened work after interruptions.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed across modern desktop operating systems. Users commonly ask questions such as "Where did my window go?", "Is the application still open?", or "Why can't I find my document?" Although operating systems provide taskbars, docks, and window previews, users still spend considerable time searching for obscured or minimized windows, particularly during complex multitasking sessions.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on window discoverability rather than switching behaviour.
*   Extends OS-026 by examining the visibility of active workspaces.
*   Frequently observed during prolonged desktop usage involving multiple applications.
*   Likely contributes to future behavioural patterns related to **Workspace Visibility**, **Desktop Awareness**, and **Attention Management**.
