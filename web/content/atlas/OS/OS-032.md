Many digital tasks require users to move repeatedly between different applications. Every transition requires users to recall previous information, identify where they left off, and mentally re-establish the task context. Frequent context switching increases cognitive effort, interrupts concentration, and reduces overall task efficiency, even when the window navigation itself is fast.

**Human Impact**

*   Loss of concentration.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Higher likelihood of mistakes.
*   Longer task completion times.
*   Mental fatigue during extended work sessions.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Researching while writing reports.
*   Programming while consulting documentation.
*   Responding to messages during focused work.
*   Managing multiple business applications.
*   Conducting online meetings while taking notes.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed across desktop operating systems regardless of the applications being used. Although operating systems enable rapid application switching, users often require several seconds to mentally reorient themselves after each transition. The disruption becomes more significant as the number of active applications and simultaneous tasks increases.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on the cognitive impact of multitasking rather than interface navigation.
*   Extends the study of desktop productivity from physical workspace management to mental workload.
*   Forms a bridge between operating system interaction and human attention research.
*   Future behavioural pattern: **Context Switching & Cognitive Load**.
