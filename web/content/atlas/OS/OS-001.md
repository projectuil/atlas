When people use a computer for the first time, they often do not understand the purpose of the desktop. They may recognize it as the first screen that appears after starting the computer but are uncertain whether it is a workspace, a storage location, a home screen, or simply a background. Without understanding its purpose, users struggle to organize their work, locate applications, and navigate the operating system effectively.

**Human Impact**

*   Increased confusion during initial computer use.
*   Slower understanding of the operating system.
*   Difficulty locating applications and files.
*   Increased dependence on external guidance.
*   Reduced confidence while interacting with the computer.

**Context**

Commonly occurs:

*   During first-time computer use.
*   Among beginners learning Windows, macOS, or Linux.
*   During digital literacy training.
*   When transitioning from smartphones or tablets to desktop computers.
*   When using an unfamiliar operating system.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed among first-time computer users and individuals with limited digital experience. Many users perceive the desktop only as the screen displayed after login without understanding its functional role within the operating system. Questions such as _"What is the desktop used for?"_, _"Can I save things here?"_, or _"Why are there icons on this screen?"_ are commonly encountered during introductory computer training. Although modern operating systems present the desktop as the primary workspace, its purpose is rarely explained explicitly, requiring users to gradually infer its role through experience.

**Research Notes**

*   Represents one of the earliest human frictions encountered while learning an operating system.
*   Focuses on conceptual understanding rather than navigation or usability.
*   Forms the foundation for later Human Frictions involving applications, files, and system organization.
*   Likely contributes to future behavioural patterns related to **Operating System Mental Models**.
