Users often understand individual operating system components, such as files, applications, memory, storage, drivers, and user accounts, but struggle to understand how these components interact as a complete system. Without a holistic mental model, users find it difficult to predict how changes in one component may affect another, limiting their ability to diagnose problems and develop confidence using the operating system.

**Human Impact**

*   Fragmented understanding of operating systems.
*   Difficulty predicting system behaviour.
*   Reduced effectiveness during troubleshooting.
*   Increased cognitive load.
*   Continued dependence on external guidance.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Learning operating system architecture.
*   Troubleshooting complex issues.
*   Installing software or hardware.
*   Studying computer science or IT.
*   Transitioning from beginner to intermediate computer use.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed as users gain experience with computers. While they gradually learn the purpose of individual components, they often fail to understand how those components communicate and depend on one another. Questions such as **"How does the operating system know which application opens a file?"** or **"Why does installing a driver affect my hardware?"** demonstrate an incomplete understanding of system interactions.
