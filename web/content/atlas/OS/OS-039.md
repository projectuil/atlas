Modern operating systems manage applications as independent entities rather than as components of a single task. During complex work, users often distribute related information across browsers, documents, terminals, messaging platforms, notes, and file explorers. Although these resources collectively support one objective, the operating system does not recognize their relationship. As a result, users must manually maintain and reconstruct the task context throughout the workflow.

**Human Impact**

*   Increased cognitive load while managing complex tasks.
*   Time spent locating task-related resources.
*   Frequent loss of workflow continuity.
*   Reduced productivity during long-term projects.
*   Higher mental effort when resuming unfinished work.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Conducting cybersecurity investigations.
*   Developing software applications.
*   Performing academic research.
*   Preparing technical reports.
*   Managing business or engineering projects.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users rarely complete complex work within a single application. A cybersecurity analyst may simultaneously use SIEM dashboards, browser tabs, terminal sessions, documentation, spreadsheets, and communication platforms while investigating one incident. Similarly, a researcher may reference articles, write notes, browse sources, and organize files across multiple applications. Although these resources belong to the same objective, operating systems manage them independently, leaving users responsible for preserving the overall task context.

**Research Notes**

*   Demonstrates the gap between human task-oriented thinking and application-oriented operating system design.
*   Existing operating systems primarily organize resources by application rather than by user objective.
*   Establishes a foundation for future research into task-centric operating systems and intelligent workspace management.
