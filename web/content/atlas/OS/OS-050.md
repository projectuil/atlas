Contemporary operating systems are fundamentally organized around computing resources such as files, applications, processes, memory, and hardware devices. Humans, however, organize their activities around goals, projects, decisions, and outcomes. This architectural mismatch requires users to continuously translate human work into computer-oriented structures. Rather than supporting work as the primary unit of organization, operating systems expect users to adapt their workflows to the system's internal model.

**Human Impact**

*   Continuous manual organization of digital work.
*   Increased cognitive burden.
*   Fragmented user experiences across applications.
*   Reduced support for complex, long-term knowledge work.
*   Limited alignment between computing environments and human thinking.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Conducting multidisciplinary research.
*   Managing enterprise operations.
*   Developing software systems.
*   Coordinating cybersecurity investigations.
*   Working across interconnected digital ecosystems.

**Evidence / Observation**

Knowledge workers define success by completing objectives rather than interacting with applications. Nevertheless, operating systems expose files, windows, processes, and storage as their primary organizational model. Users must therefore bridge the gap between human objectives and computer structures by manually integrating tools, resources, and information into coherent workflows. This architectural mismatch becomes increasingly significant as digital work grows in complexity.

**Research Notes**

*   Represents the overarching architectural Human Friction for the Operating Systems category.
*   Synthesizes previous observations concerning task context, project workspaces, intent awareness, adaptive workflows, unified work intelligence, explainability, collaboration, and human-centered optimization.
*   Suggests that future operating systems should organize computing around **human work**, making projects, goals, and intentions first-class system entities alongside applications and files.
