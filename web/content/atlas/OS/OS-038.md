Virtual desktops allow users to separate different tasks into independent workspaces without requiring additional physical monitors. However, many users struggle to understand, organize, and navigate between virtual desktops. Applications are often opened in the wrong workspace, users forget where tasks are located, and switching between desktops introduces additional cognitive effort rather than simplifying workflow management.

**Human Impact**

*   Confusion about workspace locations.
*   Time lost searching across desktops.
*   Underutilization of virtual desktop features.
*   Increased mental workload.
*   Reduced multitasking efficiency.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Managing multiple projects.
*   Separating personal and professional work.
*   Programming across different environments.
*   Research involving many applications.
*   Using laptops with limited screen space.

**Evidence / Observation**

Modern operating systems provide virtual desktop functionality to improve workspace organization. Despite its availability, adoption remains relatively low among many users. Those who do use virtual desktops frequently encounter challenges remembering where applications are located, maintaining consistent workspace organization, and switching efficiently between task-specific environments.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on logical workspace separation rather than physical displays.
*   Builds upon earlier Human Frictions related to multitasking and workspace organization.
*   Forms the foundation for research into intelligent workspace automation and adaptive desktop environments.
*   Future behavioural pattern: **Logical Workspace Management**.
