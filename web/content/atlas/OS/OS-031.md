Modern operating systems provide split-screen, window snapping, and tiling features to simplify multitasking. However, many users are unaware of these capabilities or struggle to use them effectively. Instead of leveraging built-in layout tools, users manually resize and reposition windows, resulting in slower workflows and inefficient workspace organization.

**Human Impact**

*   Increased manual window adjustments.
*   Reduced productivity.
*   Poor utilization of multitasking features.
*   Extra effort organizing the workspace.
*   Underuse of operating system capabilities.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Comparing documents side-by-side.
*   Researching while writing.
*   Programming with documentation.
*   Working on spreadsheets and browsers simultaneously.
*   Editing content across multiple applications.

**Evidence / Observation**

Many desktop operating systems include features such as Snap Layouts, Split View, and window tiling. Despite their availability, users frequently continue to resize windows manually because they are unaware of these features or find them unintuitive. As a result, built-in productivity capabilities remain significantly underutilized.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on adoption of built-in workspace management features.
*   Distinct from OS-029 (workspace organization) and OS-030 (screen space limitations).
*   Future behavioural pattern: **Feature Discoverability**.
