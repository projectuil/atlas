Users are frequently interrupted by notifications, messages, meetings, phone calls, or other external events while working. After the interruption ends, they often struggle to remember what they were doing, which applications were relevant, and where they left off. Reconstructing the previous task context requires additional time and mental effort, reducing workflow continuity.

**Human Impact**

*   Loss of productivity.
*   Increased cognitive effort.
*   Longer task completion times.
*   Greater likelihood of forgetting unfinished work.
*   Reduced focus after interruptions.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Responding to messages during focused work.
*   Attending meetings.
*   Receiving phone calls.
*   Switching to urgent tasks.
*   Returning to work after extended breaks.

**Evidence / Observation**

This behaviour is consistently observed across desktop operating systems. Although applications generally preserve their state, operating systems provide limited support for helping users reconstruct their previous workflow. Users often spend several minutes reopening documents, identifying active windows, rereading content, and recalling unfinished tasks before productive work resumes.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on workflow recovery after interruptions.
*   Extends OS-032 from context switching to interruption recovery.
*   Supports future behavioural pattern research on **Task Resumption**.
