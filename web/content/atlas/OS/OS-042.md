As users work, operating systems continuously adapt to changing conditions by reallocating resources, suspending background processes, displaying notifications, initiating updates, reconnecting hardware, or modifying application behavior. However, these adaptations often occur without clearly communicating their impact. Users therefore struggle to predict how the operating system will behave as workloads, devices, or environmental conditions change.

**Human Impact**

*   Uncertainty during critical tasks.
*   Reduced trust in automated system behavior.
*   Workflow interruptions caused by unexpected system actions.
*   Increased hesitation when performing resource-intensive work.
*   Greater reliance on manual intervention.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Connecting or disconnecting external devices.
*   Switching between battery and AC power.
*   Running resource-intensive workloads.
*   Experiencing network interruptions.
*   Receiving operating system updates.
*   Working across changing environments.

**Evidence / Observation**

Modern operating systems dynamically adjust their behavior in response to workload and environmental changes. Examples include power management, background synchronization, automatic updates, memory compression, and device reconfiguration. Although these mechanisms improve overall efficiency, users often cannot anticipate when they will occur or how they will affect ongoing work, creating uncertainty during complex workflows.

**Research Notes**

*   Focuses on system predictability rather than system performance.
*   Investigates the gap between automated operating system decisions and user expectations.
*   Establishes a foundation for future research into transparent and anticipatory operating systems capable of explaining adaptive behavior before it affects users.
