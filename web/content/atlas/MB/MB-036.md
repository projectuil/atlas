Mobile devices serve as universal capture tools. Camera photos, memes, critical document scans, and accidental screenshots are typically dumped into a single, chronological "Camera Roll." Users lack the tools and screen space to effectively triage, tag, and sort this massive, continuous influx of disparate media.

**Human Impact**

*   Essential documents (like a picture of a passport) become buried beneath thousands of dog photos.
*   Embarrassment when scrolling through personal photos to find a professional screenshot during a meeting.
*   Chronic digital hoarding because sorting is mechanically too difficult on a touch screen.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Trying to locate a specific receipt screenshot taken six months ago.
*   Attempting to organize vacation photos into a shared album while commuting.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users frequently rely on the device's AI search (e.g., typing "receipt" into the photo app) rather than folders, but fail when the AI cannot index abstract or poorly lit items, leaving the asset lost.

**Research Notes**

Examines the failure of chronological sorting as the primary architecture for diverse media capture.
