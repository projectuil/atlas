There is no universal standard for where settings should reside in mobile apps. Some place them in a left-side hamburger menu, some under a profile avatar on the top right, some in a bottom navigation tab, and others within the global OS settings app, creating a frustrating scavenger hunt for the user.

**Human Impact**

*   Wasted time clicking through multiple menus to change a simple toggle.
*   Abandonment of personalization efforts due to navigational friction.
*   Reliance on Google searches just to find how to turn off an app's notifications.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Trying to locate privacy controls, dark mode toggles, or notification preferences in a newly downloaded app.
*   Attempting to delete an account or cancel a subscription hidden deep within nested menus.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users tap their profile icon, realize it only opens a photo editor, back out, tap the hamburger menu, find a "Preferences" tab, realize it only contains language options, and finally give up trying to find the "Mute" setting.

**Research Notes**

Examines the failure of platform-agnostic UI frameworks to enforce standard administrative pathways.
