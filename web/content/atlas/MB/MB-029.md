The swipe-to-dismiss gesture for notifications is highly sensitive. Once a notification is dismissed, standard mobile OS interfaces offer no obvious "undo" or easily accessible notification history, permanently severing the user's link to the underlying task or message.

**Human Impact**

*   Missed meetings, forgotten messages, or ignored security alerts.
*   Frantic searching through multiple apps trying to figure out which one triggered the dismissed alert.
*   Anxiety over potentially missing critical but ephemeral information.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Rapidly clearing a cluttered lock screen of marketing spam and accidentally swiping a genuine alert.
*   Unlocking a phone while distracted and inadvertently clearing the notification tray.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users frequently experience the "phantom notification" panic: they see a badge for a split second before a stray thumb clears it, prompting them to open email, texts, and social apps sequentially to hunt for the lost message.

**Research Notes**

Points to the lack of temporal buffering and undo functionality in OS notification centers.
