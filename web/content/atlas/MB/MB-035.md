When a mobile app crashes due to memory limits or bugs, the OS typically restarts it from the default launch screen. Mobile apps frequently lack auto-save mechanisms for active states (like half-written comments, selected filters, or video playback positions), permanently wiping out the user's progress.

**Human Impact**

*   Extreme user hostility toward the app developers.
*   Refusal to undertake complex or time-consuming tasks within that specific app again.
*   Wasted time retracing navigation steps to get back to the pre-crash location.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Using heavy multimedia editors or AR applications on older hardware.
*   The OS forces an update or background kill while the user is actively multitasking.

**Evidence / Observation**

A user is deeply filtering a clothing app by size, color, brand, and price. The app crashes. Upon reopening, the user is dumped onto the homepage and abandons the purchase entirely rather than rebuilding the filter parameters.

**Research Notes**

Focuses on the lack of continuous, granular state preservation APIs in mobile application development.
