Unlike desktop computers locked in controlled office environments, mobile devices are used in glaring sunlight, on shaky trains, walking in the rain, and while carrying groceries. Mobile UIs that demand two hands, precise taps, or high-contrast vision fail instantly when environmental conditions shift.

**Human Impact**

*   Inability to interact with critical tools while in transit.
*   Accidental inputs caused by walking or vehicle motion.
*   Danger caused by UIs demanding too much visual attention while the user is navigating the physical world.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Attempting to respond to an urgent message one-handed while holding onto a subway pole.
*   Trying to read a low-contrast app interface outdoors in direct sunlight.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users frequently drop their phones or pause entirely on a busy sidewalk because an app requires a complex, two-handed pinch-to-zoom gesture that cannot be safely executed while walking.

**Research Notes**

Emphasizes the necessity of "situational disability" testing and robust one-handed operational modes in mobile UX.
