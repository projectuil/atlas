Over a multi-year lifecycle, a user's phone accumulates hundreds of single-use apps, thousands of photos, and countless unread badges. Because the OS lacks proactive, intelligent cleanup mechanisms, the device decays into an unmanageable labyrinth of digital hoarding.

**Human Impact**

*   Feeling overwhelmed by one's own device.
*   Giving up on organizing home screens, relying entirely on search.
*   Visual fatigue from pages of disorganized icons and constant red notification dots.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Transferring a backup from an old phone to a new one, carrying over years of legacy clutter.
*   Traveling and downloading a dozen local transit/food apps that are never deleted afterward.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users frequently have 4+ pages of apps on their home screen but only interact with the 6 apps on their dock, leaving the rest as a digital graveyard they are too overwhelmed to sort or delete.

**Research Notes**

Underlines the concept of "Digital Entropy" and the failure of manual user organization at scale.
