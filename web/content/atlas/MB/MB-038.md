The human finger is a blunt instrument compared to a mouse cursor. Editing tasks that require pixel-level or character-level precision (cropping an image strictly to a border, moving a cursor to the middle of a misspelled word, adjusting a slider by 1%) are mechanically infuriating on standard touch screens.

**Human Impact**

*   Abandonment of creative or detailed work on mobile platforms.
*   Frequent "fat-finger" errors leading to incorrect data entry.
*   Eyestrain and physical tension when attempting delicate maneuvers.

**Context**

Commonly occurs when:

*   Trimming the start and end points of a video clip in a mobile editor.
*   Trying to fix a single typo in a crowded block of hyperlinked text.

**Evidence / Observation**

Users attempting to drag a timeline slider to exactly 00:15 seconds will routinely jump between 00:14 and 00:16, unable to achieve the micro-movement required, eventually settling for an imperfect edit.

**Research Notes**

Highlights the "fat finger problem" and the need for software-assisted snapping or magnifying paradigms in touch interfaces.
