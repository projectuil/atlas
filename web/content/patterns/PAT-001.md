**Pattern**

As digital information grows and ages, people gradually lose the contextual understanding needed to locate, interpret, and effectively use that information. Although the information remains available, the surrounding knowledge—such as why it was created, where it was stored, how it was organized, and how it relates to other information—fades over time.

This recurring pattern reflects a fundamental limitation of human memory interacting with increasingly complex digital environments. It appears consistently across multiple Human Frictions involving file management, project organization, navigation, and information retrieval.

**Human Impact**

This pattern contributes to:

*   Increased cognitive load while recalling information.
*   Longer search times for existing content.
*   Confusion caused by missing or forgotten context.
*   Duplicate work because existing information cannot be located or understood.
*   Reduced productivity during information-intensive tasks.
*   Interrupted workflows as users attempt to reconstruct lost context.

**Evidence**

This Pattern is currently supported by the following Human Frictions:

*   **FF-001** – Users struggle to locate downloaded files after downloading them.
*   **FF-002** – Large collections of files and folders become difficult to understand and navigate.
*   **FF-003** – Users forget or no longer understand file and folder names over time.
*   **FF-004** – Navigating large software projects becomes increasingly difficult as contextual understanding declines.

Across these observations, the recurring relationship is the gradual loss of contextual knowledge rather than the loss of the information itself. Each Human Friction demonstrates how preserved data becomes less useful when its surrounding context fades.

**Research Notes**

Current evidence suggests that Context Loss Over Time is a foundational behavioural pattern affecting digital information management. As additional Human Frictions are documented across other ATLAS categories, this Pattern should continue to be evaluated for broader applicability.

Future validation should determine whether this Pattern extends beyond file management into areas such as communication, learning, authentication, collaboration, and knowledge work.
