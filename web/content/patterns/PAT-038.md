**Pattern**

Users repeatedly switch between websites, tabs, devices, and information sources while pursuing a single objective, increasing cognitive load and reducing continuity. Because single complex tasks (e.g., travel planning, research, online shopping) are rarely self-contained within a single website, users must orchestrate multi-window browser states manually.

This pattern captures the mental strain of cross-application multitasking. When switching between fragmented sources, users lose context, struggle to resume interrupted workflows, accumulate excessive open browser tabs, and expend substantial cognitive energy re-orienting themselves upon each context transition.

**Human Impact**

*   Severe cognitive fatigue and mental clutter caused by managing multiple open tabs.
*   Frequent loss of working memory and context when switching between sites or devices.
*   Inability to seamlessly resume interrupted work after leaving a web tool.
*   Increased time and effort required to complete multi-step online tasks.
*   Higher risk of making errors during information synthesis across sites.

**Evidence**

This Pattern is currently supported by the following Human Frictions:

*   **WI-008** – Users struggle to compare information presented across multiple websites.
*   **WI-020** – Users struggle to complete tasks because information and actions are distributed across multiple websites.
*   **WI-021** – Users struggle to manage multiple browser tabs while performing a single task.
*   **WI-022** – Users struggle to resume unfinished work after leaving and returning to a website.
*   **WI-024** – Users struggle to maintain context while switching between multiple websites during a single task.
*   **WI-042** – Users struggle to organize information collected from multiple websites during research.
*   **WI-043** – Users struggle to continue interrupted web tasks across different devices.

Across these observations, the core issue is the browser's document-centric architecture failing to support continuous, multi-resource task workspaces.

**Research Notes**

Highlights the mismatch between tabbed browser interfaces and goal-oriented human tasks. Future web UX research must explore task-centric workspace grouping, seamless cross-device session handoffs, and ambient context restoration.
