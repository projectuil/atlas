**Pattern**

Digital information rarely exists in isolation. Files, documents, and other digital resources frequently depend on, reference, influence, or interact with one another. However, these relationships are often invisible to users. While digital systems store individual objects effectively, they provide limited support for helping users perceive the interconnected structure that exists between them.

This recurring pattern reflects a fundamental limitation in human interaction with digital ecosystems. Users naturally perceive individual files but struggle to understand the hidden relationships that connect them. As these relationships become more numerous and complex, users experience increasing difficulty predicting dependencies, tracing information flow, and understanding how changes to one resource may affect others.

**Human Impact**

This pattern contributes to:

*   Increased cognitive load while understanding projects.
*   Difficulty identifying dependencies between files.
*   Greater uncertainty before modifying information.
*   Increased risk of unintended consequences.
*   Reduced transparency in complex digital ecosystems.
*   Slower navigation through interconnected information.

**Evidence**

This Pattern is currently supported by the following Human Frictions:

*   **FF-028** – Users struggle to determine whether all required files for a task have been collected.
*   **FF-033** – Users struggle to understand how one file influenced another.
*   **FF-036** – Users struggle to understand whether a file depends on external resources.
*   **FF-039** – Users struggle to understand the consequences of modifying a file.
*   **FF-043** – Users struggle to understand how information flows across project files.
*   **FF-045** – Users struggle to determine whether a file can be modified safely.

Across these observations, the recurring relationship is that users struggle to perceive and reason about the connections that exist between digital resources. Each Human Friction demonstrates that while relationships exist within digital systems, they are often implicit rather than visible, requiring users to mentally reconstruct connections that the system does not explicitly communicate.

**Research Notes**

Current evidence suggests that **Relationship Blindness** is a foundational behavioural pattern affecting how humans understand interconnected digital information. Unlike patterns focused on memory or organizational complexity, this Pattern emphasizes the limited human ability to perceive hidden relationships between digital resources.

Future validation should determine whether this Pattern extends beyond file management into areas such as software architecture, databases, operating systems, cybersecurity, knowledge graphs, artificial intelligence, collaborative platforms, and enterprise information systems.
