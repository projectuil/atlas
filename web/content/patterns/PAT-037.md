**Pattern**

A person's digital identity becomes distributed across numerous independent services, creating fragmented account management and identity awareness. Users accumulate dozens of accounts registered via disparate methods—including standalone emails, federated Social SSOs, and legacy logins—without a clear index of their credentials or active permissions.

This pattern illustrates the identity maintenance burden imposed on users. As identity spreads across disconnected platforms, users lose track of which accounts they own, struggle to confirm active session states, face complex multi-step recovery flows upon forgetting credentials, and gradually lose oversight of their overall online footprint.

**Human Impact**

*   Frustration caused by continuous trial-and-error authentication attempts.
*   Frequent account lockouts and abandoned recovery processes.
*   Unintended creation of duplicate accounts, resulting in siloed data.
*   Vulnerability to security breaches due to credential reuse across sprawling services.
*   A sense of alienation regarding control over personal online identities.

**Evidence**

This Pattern is currently supported by the following Human Frictions:

*   **WI-027** – Users struggle to remember which account they created for different websites.
*   **WI-028** – Users struggle to determine whether they are currently signed in to a website.
*   **WI-031** – Users struggle to manage multiple online accounts across different websites.
*   **WI-032** – Users struggle to recover access to online accounts after forgetting login credentials.
*   **WI-043** – Users struggle to continue interrupted web tasks across different devices.
*   **WI-050** – Users gradually lose awareness of how their online identity becomes distributed across interconnected web services.

Across these observations, the fragmentation of web authentication mechanisms creates widespread identity management failure for end-users.

**Research Notes**

Identifies identity management as a core friction point in modern web usage. Emphasizes the need for unified, user-owned sovereign identity layers and transparent federated access tracking.
