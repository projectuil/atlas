**Pattern**

Users continuously make decisions about authentication methods, account selection, verification processes, permissions, and identity-related actions while interacting with digital systems.

This high-density pattern describes the decision-making processes required of users when selecting sign-in routes (SSO vs. manual), toggling between multiple accounts, assessing security options, and resolving permission barriers.

**Human Impact**

This pattern contributes to:

*   Decision fatigue from evaluating multiple login methods (Social login vs. Passkey vs. Password).
*   Hesitation when prompted to grant OAuth permissions during federated sign-ins.
*   Workflow friction when toggling between active accounts or cross-device sessions.
*   Disorientation caused by post-submission barriers or unexpected re-authentications.
*   Misunderstanding the distinction between login state (authentication) and user rights (authorization).

**Evidence**

This Pattern is currently supported by the following Human Frictions:

*   **AU-005** — Users struggle to choose between multiple authentication options during sign-in.
*   **AU-008** — Users struggle to understand the permissions requested during social login options.
*   **AU-009** — Users struggle to navigate single sign-on (SSO) redirects and account selection screens.
*   **AU-015** — Users struggle to remember whether an account was created using email, password, or a third-party service.
*   **AU-020** — Users struggle to transition from traditional passwords to passkeys or biometric login.
*   **AU-021** — Users struggle to manage permission grants given to third-party applications.
*   **AU-025** — Users struggle to evaluate security risks associated with public or shared device authentication.
*   **AU-030** — Users struggle to recognize when they are already authenticated across connected services.
*   **AU-033** — Users struggle to determine which authentication method is the most secure for their needs.
*   **AU-041** — Users struggle to continue lengthy forms after accidental interruptions or session expiration.
*   **AU-043** — Users struggle to understand why completed forms require additional verification before submission.
*   **AU-044** — Users struggle to manage authentication while using multiple devices simultaneously.
*   **AU-045** — Users struggle to understand the relationship between authentication, authorization, and user permissions.

Across these observations, the recurring relationship is the cognitive load of evaluating identity routes, permissions, and access rules during system interactions.

**Research Notes**

This broad pattern highlights that modern login screens present too many choices without adequate guidance, causing decision paralysis.

Future validation should focus on smart default recommendations and streamlined unified login mechanisms to simplify decision trees.
