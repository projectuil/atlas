**Pattern**

As digital information grows in volume, diversity, and organizational complexity, the effort required to understand, navigate, and manage that information increases disproportionately. While digital systems can store virtually unlimited information, human cognitive capacity remains relatively constant. This growing imbalance causes previously manageable collections of files and projects to become increasingly difficult to organize, maintain, and understand.

This recurring pattern reflects a fundamental scalability challenge in digital information management. Information grows continuously, but the mechanisms available to help users manage that growth often remain static. As a result, users experience progressively higher cognitive demands as their digital environments expand.

**Human Impact**

This pattern contributes to:

*   Increased cognitive load while managing large information collections.
*   Longer navigation and search times.
*   Reduced organizational consistency as projects expand.
*   Greater difficulty maintaining long-term digital workspaces.
*   Increased likelihood of information overload.
*   Reduced productivity in complex digital environments.

**Evidence**

This Pattern is currently supported by the following Human Frictions:

*   **FF-002** – Too many files and folders make projects difficult to understand.
*   **FF-004** – Navigating large software projects is difficult.
*   **FF-009** – Users create inconsistent folder structures for similar projects.
*   **FF-021** – Users struggle to identify files that are no longer actively used.
*   **FF-025** – Users gradually lose understanding of their own file collections over time.

Across these observations, the recurring relationship is that increasing information volume introduces increasing organizational and cognitive complexity. Each Human Friction demonstrates that as digital collections expand, users experience a corresponding decline in their ability to effectively understand and manage them.

**Research Notes**

Current evidence suggests that **Complexity Growth** is a foundational behavioural pattern influencing long-term digital information management. Unlike patterns focused on memory or context, this Pattern emphasizes the natural increase in management complexity that accompanies information growth.

Future validation should determine whether this Pattern extends beyond file management into areas such as operating systems, web applications, communication platforms, knowledge management systems, software development, and collaborative work environments.
