__1\. Purpose__

The Human Friction Template defines the official structure for documenting Human Frictions within the ATLAS Research Repository\.

Its purpose is to ensure every Human Friction is:

- Consistent 
- Easy to read 
- Easy to review 
- Easy to maintain 
- Searchable 
- Research\-ready 
- Scalable for long\-term research 

This template emphasizes __observing human problems__ rather than proposing solutions\.

Every accepted Human Friction within ATLAS should follow this template\.

__2\. Core Principle__

A Human Friction documents __one recurring human problem__\.

It should answer:

- What is the problem? 
- Why does it matter? 
- Where does it occur? 
- What evidence supports it? 
- What additional research should be conducted? 

The document should remain objective and avoid discussing possible solutions\.

__4\. Human Friction Structure__

Every Human Friction should contain the following sections\.

__Problem__

Describe the recurring human problem clearly and objectively\.

Focus only on the problem itself\.

Do not propose or discuss solutions\.

__Human Impact__

Describe how this problem affects people\.

Possible impacts include:

- Time loss 
- Confusion 
- Increased cognitive load 
- Frustration 
- Errors 
- Reduced productivity 

__Context__

Describe where and when the Human Friction occurs\.

Examples:

- While organizing files 
- During authentication 
- While learning online 
- During software development 

The context should describe the environment without restricting the observation to one specific product or platform\.

__Evidence / Observation__

Document the observations supporting this Human Friction\.

Evidence may come from:

- Personal observation 
- Repeated behavior 
- User interviews 
- User testing 
- Community discussions 
- Research literature 

Evidence should describe observed behavior rather than assumptions\.

__Research Notes__

Capture information that may support future research\.

Examples include:

- Similar Human Frictions 
- Initial hypotheses 
- Questions for future investigation 
- Interesting observations 
- Related research topics 

Research Notes are exploratory and should not be treated as validated conclusions\.

__Revision History__

Maintain a record of significant document changes\.

__6\. Writing Guidelines__

Every Human Friction should:

- Describe only one recurring problem\. 
- Be written objectively\. 
- Focus on observations rather than opinions\. 
- Avoid proposing solutions\. 
- Use clear and concise language\. 
- Follow the ATLAS Naming Convention\. 
- Follow the APID Standard\. 
- Follow the Validation Classification Standard\. 
- Remain understandable to both researchers and future contributors\. 

__7\. Final Principle__

ATLAS exists to build a comprehensive understanding of recurring human problems\.

Every Human Friction should contribute one reliable observation to that mission\.

A Human Friction should be __simple enough to document quickly, detailed enough to support future research, and consistent enough to scale across thousands of observations\.__
