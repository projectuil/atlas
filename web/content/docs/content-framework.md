__ PROJECT UIL CONTENT FRAMEWORK__

__Version 1\.0__

__1\. Purpose__

The Content Framework defines __what Project UIL publishes, why it is published, and how each piece of content contributes to the long\-term mission__\.

Every public publication must belong to a defined content type\.

No exceptions\.

__2\. Content Mission__

Every piece of content should achieve __at least one__ of the following objectives:

- Document a real observation\. 
- Improve understanding of human behaviour\. 
- Encourage thoughtful discussion\. 
- Share research progress\. 
- Explain systems thinking\. 
- Build public trust in the research\. 

__3\. Content Pillars__

Project UIL has __seven core content pillars__\.

__PILLAR 01 - Atlas Observations \(AO\)__

__Purpose__

Document one Human Friction at a time\.

__Structure__

- Observation ID 
- Human Friction 
- Why it happens 
- Human impact 
- Reflection 

Example:

__ATLAS Observation \#001__

Downloaded files are difficult to find after downloading\.

__PILLAR 02 - Research Patterns \(RP\)__

__Purpose__

Connect multiple Human Frictions into one broader insight\.

Example:

Five Human Frictions reveal the same underlying problem:  
Systems expect humans to remember too much\.

__PILLAR 03 - Human Stories \(HS\)__

__Purpose__

Tell a real story that reveals a Human Friction\.

Sources may include:

- Personal experiences 
- Family 
- Friends 
- Public spaces 
- Education 
- Healthcare 
- Everyday technology 

Protect privacy by removing identifying details\.

__PILLAR 04 - Systems Thinking \(ST\)__

__Purpose__

Explain how multiple systems interact\.

Examples:

- Payments 
- Healthcare 
- Transportation 
- Education 
- Authentication 
- Government services 

Focus on relationships rather than isolated problems\.

__PILLAR 05 - Research Journal \(RJ\)__

__Purpose__

Document the journey of Project UIL\.

Examples:

- New categories added 
- Milestones reached 
- Lessons learned 
- Changes in research approach 

This is the "building in public" element\.

__PILLAR 06 - Open Questions \(OQ\)__

__Purpose__

Ask thoughtful questions\.

Not surveys\.

Not engagement bait\.

Example:

Why do digital systems expect users to adapt instead of adapting to users?

__PILLAR 07 - Milestones \(MS\)__

__Purpose__

Celebrate meaningful progress\.

Examples:

- 100 Human Frictions documented 
- New research category completed 
- New publication released 

Milestones should focus on the work, not personal achievements\.

__4\. Content Series__

Create recurring series so readers recognise patterns\.

Examples:

__ATLAS Observation__

Every observation follows the same format\.

__Weekly Research Log__

One post every week\.

Topics:

- What changed 
- What was learned 
- What's next 

__Pattern of the Month__

One deeper insight each month\.

__Human Story__

One real\-world story that highlights a broader issue\.

__System Spotlight__

A closer look at one ecosystem, such as healthcare or payments\.

__5\. Content Structure__

Every post follows this flow:

Title



Context



Observation



Insight



Reflection



Question or Closing Thought

This gives readers a familiar rhythm\.

__6\. Content Priority__

Not every idea should become a post\.

Use this priority:

__Priority__

__Publish?__

Original observation

 Yes

Verified research insight

 Yes

Project milestone

 Yes

Thoughtful question

 Yes

Random opinion

 No

Trend reaction

 Usually No

Motivational quote

 No

__7\. Content Lifecycle__

Every post moves through the same workflow:

Observation

      

Research

      

Draft

      

Review

      

Design

      

Publish

      

Discussion

      

Archive

      

Reference in future research

Nothing is published without review\.

__8\. Numbering System__

Every content type has a unique ID\.

Examples:

AO\-001

AO\-002

RP\-001

HS\-001

ST\-001

RJ\-001

OQ\-001

MS\-001

This creates an organised archive over time\.

__9\. Publishing Balance__

Avoid repeating the same content type\.

A healthy monthly mix might look like:

- 35% Atlas Observations 
- 20% Research Patterns 
- 15% Human Stories 
- 10% Systems Thinking 
- 10% Research Journal 
- 5% Open Questions 
- 5% Milestones 

This keeps the page varied while staying focused\.

__10\. Evergreen Principle__

Every post should still be worth reading months or years later\.

Ask:

__"Will this still be valuable one year from now?"__

If the answer is no, reconsider publishing it\.

__11\. Archive Strategy__

All published content should be traceable\.

Maintain a simple index:

__Post ID__

__Date__

__Category__

__Atlas Link__

__Status__

This creates a searchable history and makes it easy to reference earlier work\.

__12\. Success Criteria__

Success is __not__ measured only by likes or followers\.

Instead, ask:

- Did this post communicate one clear idea? 
- Did it reflect Project UIL's mission? 
- Did it encourage meaningful discussion? 
- Is it something worth referencing later? 

__13\. Final Principle__

__Every publication should become part of Project UIL's long\-term knowledge base-not just today's social media feed\.__
