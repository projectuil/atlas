__PROJECT UIL__

__RESEARCH STANDARDS & ATLAS METHODOLOGY__

__Version 1\.0__

__1\. Purpose__

This document defines the official research methodology followed by Project UIL for discovering, validating, documenting, maintaining, and evolving Human Frictions within the ATLAS Research Repository\.

Every Human Friction added to ATLAS must follow these standards\.

The objective is to ensure that ATLAS remains:

- Consistent 
- Reliable 
- Evidence\-based 
- Human\-centred 
- Scalable 
- Reproducible 

This methodology applies to all current and future contributors\.

__2\. Research Philosophy__

Project UIL begins with one belief:

__Technology should not begin with ideas\. Technology should begin with understanding\.__

Research always comes before:

- Design 
- Development 
- Architecture 
- Products 
- Features 

Observation always comes before solutions\.

__3\. The Research Pyramid__

Every piece of knowledge moves upward through this pyramid\.

Solutions



Architectures



Insights



Patterns



Validated Human Frictions



Observed Human Frictions



Real Human Experiences

Project UIL never skips layers\.

__4\. Definition of a Human Friction__

A __Human Friction__ is:

__A recurring difficulty, confusion, limitation, interruption, inefficiency, or cognitive burden experienced by people while interacting with digital systems, physical systems, services, environments, or everyday life\.__

A Human Friction is not:

- A feature request 
- A product idea 
- A solution 
- A complaint without context 
- A temporary bug 
- A personal preference 

__5\. The Seven Qualification Rules__

Every Human Friction must satisfy these questions\.

__Rule 1 - Human\-Centred__

Does this describe a human problem?

__Rule 2 - Observable__

Can it be observed?

Not imagined\.

__Rule 3 - Repeatable__

Does it happen repeatedly?

Not only once\.

__Rule 4 - Independent__

Is it a unique friction?

Not a duplicate\.

__Rule 5 - Solution\-Free__

Does it describe the problem without suggesting the solution?

__Rule 6 - Understandable__

Can another researcher understand it immediately?

__Rule 7 - Meaningful__

Would understanding this improve future systems?

If any answer is "No", the observation is not ready for ATLAS\.

__6\. Sources of Observation__

Project UIL accepts observations from:

- Daily life 
- Family 
- Friends 
- Education 
- Healthcare 
- Transportation 
- Banking 
- Government services 
- Technology 
- Public spaces 
- Workplaces 
- Accessibility experiences 
- Public interviews \(with appropriate attribution\) 
- Published research \(clearly identified as secondary evidence\) 

Every source should be documented where practical\.

__7\. Observation Levels__

Each entry should be tagged by evidence level\.

__Level A - Direct Observation__

Observed personally\.

Highest confidence\.

__Level B - Interview__

Reported by another person\.

Requires context\.

__Level C - Multiple Independent Reports__

Observed by many unrelated people\.

High confidence\.

__Level D - Research Derived__

Found through papers or documented studies\.

Must be cited if published externally\.

__Level E - Hypothesis__

Interesting possibility\.

Not yet accepted into ATLAS until validated\.

__8\. Observation Workflow__

Observe



Record



Validate



Remove Duplicates



Assign Category



Assign APID



Write Clearly



Review



Add to ATLAS

Every Human Friction follows this lifecycle\.

__9\. APID Assignment__

Every accepted Human Friction receives:

- Category 
- Sequential number 
- Permanent identifier 

Example:

FF\-001

MB\-021

PS\-014

TR\-006

APIDs are never reused\.

If an entry is removed, its APID remains retired\.

__10\. Writing Standard__

Every Human Friction should be written as:

Problem



Human Impact



Context \(optional\)



Notes \(optional\)

Avoid unnecessary detail\.

Use clear, neutral language\.

__11\. Duplicate Policy__

If two observations describe the same underlying problem:

- Keep the clearer version\. 
- Merge supporting notes\. 
- Record aliases if useful\. 

ATLAS should contain concepts, not duplicates\.

__12\. Category Creation__

A new category should only be created when:

- Multiple related Human Frictions exist\. 
- Existing categories no longer fit naturally\. 
- The category represents a meaningful domain\. 

Avoid creating categories for single observations\.

__13\. Research Ethics__

Project UIL researchers must:

- Respect privacy\. 
- Remove identifying information\. 
- Avoid harming individuals\. 
- Distinguish facts from assumptions\. 
- Correct mistakes openly\. 

__14\. Validation Status__

Every observation has one status\.

- Draft 
- Under Review 
- Accepted 
- Needs Revision 
- Duplicate 
- Rejected 
- Archived 

__15\. Atlas Entry Template__

Every entry should contain:

- APID 
- Category 
- Human Friction Statement 
- Observation Level 
- Validation Status 
- Date Added 
- Research Notes 
- Related APIDs \(if any\) 

__16\. Pattern Formation__

Patterns should only be created after multiple validated Human Frictions point toward the same underlying behaviour or system issue\.

Patterns are not assumptions-they emerge from evidence\.

__17\. Solution Separation Principle__

ATLAS is __not__ a product backlog\.

Do not include:

- UI designs 
- Product features 
- Architecture proposals 
- Technical implementations 

Those belong in later Project UIL documents\.

ATLAS exists to preserve the problem space\.

__18\. Continuous Improvement__

An accepted Human Friction is not immutable\.

Researchers may:

- Clarify wording\. 
- Add supporting evidence\. 
- Link related observations\. 
- Improve categorisation\. 

However, changes should preserve the original meaning\.

__19\. Research Governance__

Major changes-such as merging categories, renaming domains, or changing methodology-should be documented with a version update\.

This keeps the evolution of ATLAS transparent\.

__20\. Final Research Principle__

__Observe honestly\. Validate carefully\. Document clearly\. Build responsibly\.__

__21\. Human Friction Lifecycle__

Every Human Friction progresses through a defined lifecycle:

Real Experience

      

Raw Observation

      

Candidate Human Friction

      

Validation

      

Accepted Human Friction

      

Pattern Formation

      

Research Insight

      

Architecture Input

      

Future Product Opportunity

This is something I haven't seen formalised in most research repositories, and it captures the philosophy you've been building all along: __products are the final outcome of a disciplined research process, not the starting point\.__
