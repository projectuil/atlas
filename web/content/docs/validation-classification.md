__Document 05 - Validation Classification__

__Version 1\.0__

__1\. Purpose__

The Validation Classification Standard defines how Human Frictions progress from an initial observation to an accepted research record\.

Its purpose is to ensure that every Human Friction in ATLAS is evaluated consistently, transparently, and based on evidence rather than assumptions\.

No Human Friction should become part of ATLAS without passing this validation process\.

__2\. Core Principle__

__Observe first\. Validate second\. Accept third\.__

A Human Friction should never be accepted simply because it sounds interesting\.

It must be supported by observation and meet the ATLAS research standards\.

__4\. Validation Stages__

__Stage 1 - Idea__

A possible Human Friction is identified\.

Characteristics:

- Raw thought 
- Question 
- Personal observation 
- Not yet researched 

No APID assigned\.

__Stage 2 - Observation__

Evidence begins to appear\.

Examples:

- Personal experience 
- Direct observation 
- Repeated occurrence 
- Interviews 
- User behaviour 

Still no APID\.

__Stage 3 - Draft__

The Human Friction is documented using the official template\.

Includes:

- Problem Statement 
- Context 
- Human Impact 
- Tags 

Still editable\.

__Stage 4 - Candidate__

The observation appears valid but requires review\.

Checks include:

- Duplicate search 
- Category selection 
- Naming standard 
- Writing quality 

No APID yet\.

__Stage 5 - Under Review__

The Human Friction is reviewed against ATLAS standards\.

Questions include:

- Is it recurring? 
- Is it human\-centred? 
- Is it solution\-free? 
- Is it understandable? 
- Is it sufficiently different from existing APIDs? 

__Stage 6 - Accepted__

The Human Friction officially enters ATLAS\.

Actions:

- APID assigned 
- APID Registry updated 
- Category Registry count updated 
- GitHub committed 

This is now an official research record\.

__Stage 7 - Pattern Linked__

Later research identifies that the Human Friction contributes to one or more Patterns\.

Example:

FF\-001



PT\-002



INS\-001

__Stage 8 - Archived / Retired__

An APID is never deleted\.

Possible reasons:

- Duplicate 
- Invalid evidence 
- Merged 
- Historical reference only 

Status changes, identity remains\.

__6\. Validation Levels__

Each Human Friction should include a confidence level\.

__Level__

__Meaning__

Level 1

Single observation

Level 2

Repeated observation

Level 3

Observed across multiple people or contexts

Level 4

Strongly validated through consistent evidence

Level 5

Widely established Human Friction supported by research or extensive observation

This helps distinguish early ideas from mature findings without excluding either\.

__7\. Reasons for Rejection__

A Human Friction should not be accepted if it is:

- A proposed solution rather than a problem\. 
- A one\-time event\. 
- A duplicate of an existing APID\. 
- Too vague to understand\. 
- Based purely on opinion\. 
- Outside the scope of Project UIL\. 

Rejected observations may be retained as notes but do not receive APIDs\.

__9\. Validation Principles__

Validation should always be:

- Evidence\-driven 
- Repeatable 
- Transparent 
- Consistent 
- Open to revision 

The objective is not to prove an idea correct, but to determine whether it accurately represents a recurring Human Friction\.

__10\. Final Principle__

__Acceptance into ATLAS is earned through evidence, not creativity\. The value of the repository depends on the quality and consistency of its validation process\.__
