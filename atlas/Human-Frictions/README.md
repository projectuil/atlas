# ATLAS Human Frictions

The **Human Frictions** folder contains documented observations of recurring problems that people experience while interacting with technology.

A Human Friction represents a single observed difficulty, limitation, or inefficiency encountered during real-world interactions. These observations form the foundation of the ATLAS research repository.

Every Human Friction is documented using the official Human Friction Template and assigned a unique Atlas Problem Identifier (APID).

---

# Purpose

The purpose of this folder is to:

- Document real human problems.
- Build a structured knowledge base of Human Frictions.
- Identify recurring behavioural patterns.
- Support future Research Insights and Research Papers.
- Establish an evidence-based foundation for product innovation.

---

# Repository Structure

Human Frictions are organized by category and stored in volume documents.

Example:

Human Frictions/

- Files & Project Management/
  - FF Volume 01 (FF-001–FF-025)
  - FF Volume 02 (FF-026–FF-050)

Each Human Friction begins on a new page within its respective volume.

---

# Human Friction Structure

Every Human Friction follows the official Human Friction Template and includes:

- Metadata
- Problem
- Human Impact
- Context
- Evidence / Observation
- Research Notes
- Revision History

---

# Observation Principles

All Human Frictions should be:

- Based on real observations.
- Clearly defined and objective.
- Focused on a single recurring problem.
- Independent of proposed solutions.
- Supported by observable evidence whenever possible.

---

# Relationship to Patterns

Human Frictions document **individual observed problems**.

Patterns identify **recurring behaviours shared across multiple Human Frictions**.

As the repository grows, related Human Frictions may contribute to one or more Patterns, Research Insights, and Research Papers.

---

# Guiding Principles

> **Understand human problems before building products.**

> **Products should be built from problems, not from ideas.**

> **Never invent Human Friction. Always observe it.**
